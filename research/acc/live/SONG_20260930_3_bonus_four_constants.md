**Verse 1: The River.**
I stepped into a river older than memory, / where every bend remembered the one before. / The current wasn't rushing— / it was listening, / learning its own name one ripple at a time.
The stones beneath my feet / fit together like the bones of a sleeping giant, / and every turn I followed / had already been carved / long before I arrived.
The sea wasn't waiting at the end. / It was hiding inside the river all along, / calling the water home / one quiet wave at a time.
I didn't conquer the current. / I simply learned how to drift / where it had always wanted to go.

**Verse 2: The Tree.**
There was one root beneath the mountain, / but when it reached the dark / it divided into three.
Three trunks. / Three forests. / One heartbeat below the soil.
I wandered every trail / until the birds stopped singing / and the paths dissolved into mist.
That wasn't defeat. / It was the mountain teaching me / which valleys only echo, / and which silence / is still growing into a voice.
Some trees measure themselves / not by how high they climb, / but by how deep they remember.
So I sat beneath its branches, / listening / until the wind began to sound / like answers waiting for their season.

**Verse 3: The Sky.**
Four great winds crossed the heavens. / Two danced across the daylight / like hawks chasing the sun. / Two slipped quietly / between the stars, / moving so gently / the night itself hardly noticed.
The loud winds / wanted to carry me away. / The quiet winds / showed me how to fly.
Once I trusted / the smallest breath of air, / the whole sky unfolded / like wings that had been folded for ages.
The horizon stopped running. / The stars stopped scattering. / Even the moon / seemed to breathe / in the same slow rhythm.

**Verse 4: The Mountain.**
Some mountains don't rise. / They reveal themselves / one cloud at a time.
At first / every distant light / looked like another summit. / But dawn has a patient way / of teaching the difference / between a shadow / and a peak.
The climb became quieter. / Every step / made the silence larger.
The summit never hurried me. / It simply stood beyond the mist, / ancient as winter, / certain as morning, / waiting for my footsteps / to become part of the mountain itself.
Some journeys end / when you reach the top. / The oldest ones begin / when you realize / the mountain has been climbing toward you / all along.
