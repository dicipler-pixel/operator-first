


Light Keeps the Ledger — Matter, Direction, and the Wall Between Them







Light Keeps the Ledger

Matter, Direction, and the Wall Between Them

Jeromie Beasley · August 2026




Abstract
Every projector-valued transport family carries two registers. A metric — a Gram pairing of tangent directions at a point — protects locally and can degenerate; a ledger — a contour integral over the resolvent — needs no pairing and survives the metric's degeneration intact. This paper builds both registers from first principles, proves what each can and cannot do, and reads the physics of light and matter through them. Light, which has no rest frame and no local position operator, supplies the ledger its content: phase and integer. Matter, which has a rest frame and a finite boundary census, supplies the metric its content: a pairing that can fail. The account is then held against the full evidence on the nature of light, from the double slit to the 2025 single-atom recoiling-slit experiments, the photon-by-photon conservation of orbital angular momentum, light-by-light scattering at the LHC, and the Planck-scale bounds on dispersion and mass: in every case the experiment measures one register or the other, and the two never contradict. The duality relation \(D^2+V^2\le1\) is shown to be the same theorem as the two-band metric–curvature bound — a non-negative Gram determinant meeting rank one — so a fringe visibility is read off matter's metric. Directional capacity is measured on a lattice: sixty-four directions enter a region, a bounded number of channels survive, the count grows only slowly with depth toward an aperture-scale ceiling consistent with \(\pi R/w\), while the boundary census of the same region runs several times faster. The Kakeya question is then posed for light directly: a needle of light is a tube of width one wavelength, Kakeya at resolution \(\delta\) is exactly the question of how far superposed wave packets can concentrate, and local smoothing for the wave equation is known to imply Kakeya. Two independent null gates show that no smooth local mechanism compresses directional coverage; the field's own proofs are combinatorial at scale \(\delta\), which is what the gates say they must be. Approximation is classified by which register survives it — integers cross in the operator norm and are blind in normalized metrics — and the supply between the rails is exhibited three ways: a licence that lapses before the metric degenerates, a reciprocity that forces a perfect square, and an amplitude that dies as the square root of the distance to a wall while the contour's count never moves. Every substantive claim is proved here, verified by a gated computation printed in the appendix, cited to external published work, or explicitly labeled as a hypothesis or reading.

0. Grades, and how to read this paper

Every claim carries one of four grades: [T] proved in this paper, with the proof given; [V] verified by a gated computation printed in full in the appendix (eighteen verification scripts and one figure script; every numerical result graded [V] is produced by the gate that sits behind it); [C] cited, attributed to established, independently published literature; [H] a stated hypothesis or reading, not proved or gated. No claim rests on an unpublished companion document. A figure accompanies every relation that carries weight: seventeen in all, the data figures drawn from the gate output files rather than transcribed.


1. Introduction: what light knows that matter cannot hold

A projector-valued transport family \(\Pi(\lambda)\) supports two different questions. Locally: does a small change in \(\lambda\) cost anything? This is answered by a metric, a Gram pairing of tangent directions at \(\Pi\), and the metric can fail — it degenerates on a codimension-one locus, and a stronger licence condition it requires can fail earlier still. Globally: what survives no matter how far \(\lambda\) is carried, including through a point where the metric has already failed? This is answered by a ledger, a contour integral that needs only a rectifiable path in the resolvent set and no pairing of any kind.

The organizing claim — a reading, graded [H] wherever it is used — is that light naturally supplies the ledger's content and matter naturally supplies the metric's. Light has no rest frame and cannot be slowed into one; it is the same object at every scale a boundary census can be taken at; it carries phase and integer and nothing that a pairing at a point could hold — exactly the kind of content a contour integral, indifferent to definiteness, is built to track. Matter has a rest frame and a finite census; it is exactly what a Gram pairing, built from tangent vectors at one point, is built to protect. The potential between them exists only because the two run at different rates. That is why the ledger sits on the high side and the census on the low, and why the gap between the rails, rather than being a defect of the construction, is the supply. The two rails are registers of one construction, not two additional fields.

Underneath both registers sits one question that comes before either: is a given continuation of the operator admissible at all? The metric is the local admissibility test — whether a small step costs anything and whether the pairing that prices it still exists — and the ledger is the global continuation record, what an admissible path leaves behind that no later step can erase. That ordering, admissibility first and both registers downstream of it, is the spine the earlier architecture map already drew in three storeys: a lattice engine regularized by a shifted contour, a Riesz subspace lifted onto the Grassmannian with its metric and curvature, and the global ledger with its continuum limit [Beasley 2026b]. This paper adds the fourth storey, direction, which is where light enters.

2. The instrument

Every operator below splits as \(C=S+K\), \(S=\tfrac12(C+C^\top)\) symmetric, \(K=\tfrac12(C-C^\top)\) skew.

Proposition (the commutator identity)
\([C,C^\top]=-2[S,K]\), hence \(C\) is normal iff \(S\) and \(K\) commute. [T]

Proof. \(C^\top=S-K\). \([C,C^\top]=(S+K)(S-K)-(S-K)(S+K)=-2[S,K]\). Normality means \([C,C^\top]=0\), equivalent to \([S,K]=0\).

For the dominant singular directions this gives a stress functional \(\Phi:=\tfrac12\|[C,C^\top]\|_F^2=2\|[S,K]\|_F^2=\sin^2\theta\), used without restatement below. The symmetric sector says what the operator holds; the skew sector says what it moves; the stress says how far the two disagree.


3. The ground: projector geometry

3.1 The Dirac two-band model and its metric–curvature bound

Every \(2\times2\) Hermitian family \(h(\mathbf k)=\mathbf d(\mathbf k)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) has band projectors \(\Pi_\pm=\tfrac12(I\pm\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\). Define \(Q_\Pi(V,W)=\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)=g_\Pi-\tfrac{i}{2}\Omega_\Pi\) with \(\Omega_\Pi:=+i\,\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi[V,W])\); the sign is a convention and is stated here once.

Theorem (metric–curvature bound)
\(|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\le2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2}\), with equality always, for this two-band family. [T]

Proof. \(Q_\Pi\) is PSD Hermitian on the real span of \(\{V,W\}\), a Gram matrix, so its \(2\times2\) determinant is non-negative. Substituting \(Q_\Pi(V,V)=g_\Pi(V,V)\) and \(|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2=g_\Pi(V,W)^2+\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2\) gives the bound. Equality holds because the tangent space is one complex line.

The algebra is worth naming because it returns in Section 4.2 wearing light's clothes: a \(2\times2\) PSD Gram determinant is non-negative and vanishes exactly at rank one. Nothing more is used.


3.2 The redistribution operator

For projector \(\Pi\) and Hermitian generator \(\Omega\), \(F(\Omega,\Pi)=(I-\Pi)\Omega\Pi+\Pi\Omega(I-\Pi)\) is traceless, off-diagonal, and vanishes iff \([\Omega,\Pi]=0\). [T] It is the part of a generator that moves content across the gap; everything the ledger records passes through it.


3.3 The noncommutative anchor

For \(U,V\in U(d)\), relator \(R=UVU^\dagger V^\dagger\), defect \(\delta=\|R-I\|_F^2/d\), branch guard \(g=\min_j|\lambda_j+1|\), winding \(w=\tfrac1{2\pi}\sum_j\arg\lambda_j\):

Theorem (integrality and the domain bound)
For \(g>0\): \(w\in\mathbb Z\), \(|w|[T]

Proof. \(\det R=1\) identically, so \(\sum_j\arg\lambda_j\in2\pi\mathbb Z\) once the principal branch is fixed, which \(g>0\) guarantees; \(g>0\) means no eigenvalue sits at \(-1\), so every principal argument has modulus strictly less than \(\pi\), the sum is strictly less than \(d\pi\), and the integer \(w\) obeys \(|w|\le\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\). Section 4.3 attains it. Conjugation and rephasing leave the spectrum of \(R\) fixed. For the bound, \(\delta=\tfrac1d\sum_j|\lambda_j-1|^2=\tfrac1d\sum_j(2-2\mathrm{Re}\lambda_j)\) and \(g^2\le|\lambda_j+1|^2=2+2\mathrm{Re}\lambda_j\) for every \(j\); adding the average of the second to the first gives \(\delta+g^2\le4\), with equality iff every \(\mathrm{Re}\lambda_j\) equals the one attaining the minimum.

The clock-and-shift family saturates this at every \(d\ge3\) and is refused exactly at \(d=2\), where the relator sits at \(-I\) and the branch closes. [V] Gated to \(10^{-9}\) across \(d=3,\ldots,97\); over one thousand random unitary pairs in \(d=12\) the bound is never violated, with maximum \(2.409\) (Appendix A.1). The determinant discards exactly one wrap: \(\det R=1\) always, while the summed principal arguments keep the integer. That is why a determinant can never see the obstruction and a winding can.

3.4 A realization on published data

External data [Yi et al. 2023] calibrates a two-band tight-binding Raman-lattice model with measured Chern number \(-1.00\pm0.02\). The winding construction on this exact published band returns \(C=-0.99931\), with pointwise saturation \(|\Omega|=2\sqrt{\det g}\) holding to a mean of \(0.999958\), and quantum volume \(1.145\ge|C|\). [C]+[V] (Appendix A.2). The numerics here are computations calibrated against other people's measurements; nothing in this paper is laboratory data of its own.


3.5 Matter's own boundary count, and the scale it forces

Matter is a gap wearing a boundary. A gapped-vacuum boundary census obeys an area law while a gapless one floods it as \(L\log L\) [Gioev–Klich 2006]; condensation is the appearance of the gap, not a change of density; a gas fails non-modally, in the transient, before any mode does [Trefethen–Embree]. [C] Three scale-free classifiers then separate the states of matter with no length in them: distance from normality and what holds it there; census occupancy against the geometric cap; and which records cross a wall — amplitude dies, integers persist (Section 8.5 measures the third of these). [H]

Theorem (conditional closure of the scale dictionary)
Holding \(\hbar,c,\alpha\) fixed forces the Bohr-radius identity \(a_0=\hbar/(\alpha mc)\) to scale oppositely in length and mass: a model scaling length as \((1+z)\) forces mass as \((1+z)^{-1}\) and time as \((1+z)\); \(e^2=\alpha\hbar c\) is fixed; every dimensionless invariant checks; and under the additional model assumption \(M_{\rm Pl}\propto m\), propagating it forces \(G\propto(1+z)^2\), leaving \(\alpha_G=Gm^2/\hbar c\) invariant. [T]

Proof. \(a_0 m=\hbar/(\alpha c)\) is a fixed product, so \(a_0\propto(1+z)\) iff \(m\propto(1+z)^{-1}\). With \(\hbar\) fixed, \(E=\hbar/t\) and \(E=mc^2\) give \(t\propto m^{-1}\propto(1+z)\). \(M_{\rm Pl}^2=\hbar c/G\) with \(M_{\rm Pl}\propto m\) gives \(G\propto m^{-2}\propto(1+z)^2\), and \(Gm^2\) is then invariant.

Given fixed \(\hbar,c,\alpha\) and the length assignment, the dictionary is unique: any assignment scaling size and mass together changes a measured rod. Whether nature realizes the length assignment is a separate question the theorem does not touch. Light has no term in it. A massless carrier has nothing for \((1+z)\) to multiply, which is the operator-side reading of Section 4.4.


3.6 The continuum limit

The discrete second-difference operator \(\Delta_\delta\psi=(\psi_{n+1}-2\psi_n+\psi_{n-1})/\delta^2\) converges to \(\psi''\) in \(L^2\) at order \(O(\delta^2)\). [T] Gated (Appendix A.13): measured convergence order \(1.94\to1.999\) per halving of \(\delta\), across \(N=10\) to \(640\). [V] The lattice derivative the constructions use has this continuum consistency; whether each downstream observable has a controlled continuum limit is a separate question, addressed where it arises.


3.7 The subspace is the vehicle; the principal angles grade it

Proposition (admissibility, metric, ledger)
For a rank-\(k\) Riesz projector \(\Pi_0\) carried to \(\Pi\) along an admissible path, the subspace persistence \(\tfrac1k\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_0\Pi)=\tfrac1k\sum_i\cos^2\theta_i\) is the mean squared cosine of the principal angles between the two subspaces, and reduces to the single-vector overlap only when \(k=1\); for \(k>1\) a single mode can lose its overlap while the carrier subspace stays substantially persistent. Two directions are distinct channels iff \(\sin^2\theta\ge\varepsilon\); the same angles that separate channels grade the persistence of the carrier. [T]

Proof. With orthonormal bases \(Q_0,Q\) of the two subspaces, \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_0\Pi)=\|Q_0^\dagger Q\|_F^2=\sum_i\sigma_i^2\) and the singular values of \(Q_0^\dagger Q\) are the cosines of the principal angles. For \(k=1\) the single singular value is the vector overlap. The channel criterion is Section 5.1's dictionary read on the same angles.

Gated (Appendix A.17) on a symmetric base with a skew deformation, tracking the three lowest-real-part eigenvalues: at deformation \(\varepsilon=1\) the best single-vector overlap is \(0.60\) while the subspace persistence is \(0.82\); through a collision with the fourth eigenvalue at \(\varepsilon=2\) the vector overlap sits at \(0.56\) and the subspace at \(0.63\), recovering to \(0.69\) beyond it; the three principal angles keep their order throughout. [V] The vectors inside a subspace reshuffle; the subspace carries on. That is why the ledger is written on projectors and not on modes, and why Section 5.1 counts channels as a rank and not as a list of rays.

4. What light is, on the evidence

The record on light runs from Newton's corpuscles and Huygens' waves through Young's fringes, Fresnel's spot, Foucault's slower light in water, Maxwell, Planck, Einstein's threshold, Compton's recoil, the 1927 recoiling slit, Dirac's field, quantum electrodynamics, the 1986 anticorrelation of a single photon at a beam splitter with fringes from the same source, and delayed choice. [C] Every step of it measures one of two things: a click — a localized interaction registered by matter, whose detector degrees of freedom supply the local frame and the record — or an interference, which needs a phase carried unbroken around a loop. This section holds the newest evidence — 2024 to 2026 — against the two registers and finds that each experiment reads one register cleanly and never both at once.

4.1 No rest frame is no position operator

The metric is a Gram pairing of tangent vectors at a point. A photon has no such point: there is no Lorentz-covariant local position operator for a massless spin-one field [Newton–Wigner 1949], and the position-space wavefunction that does exist is normalized by energy density, not probability density [Bialynicki-Birula 1996], with the two standard constructions unitarily equivalent [Federico–Jauslin 2022]. [C] That is a ledger-type object — an integral over the field — rather than a metric-type object, a pairing at a location. The reason light cannot be localized is the reason the contour integral in Section 8.4 needs no definiteness: the same absence of a rest frame, seen from the two ends. The claim is about which register the invariants of this paper live in; a photon has local field quantities, correlations and a stress tensor like anything else, and none of them is a pairing that survives a wall.


4.2 Complementarity is a Gram determinant

The 1927 recoiling slit was run in 2025 with single atoms as the slits, independently by two groups [Fedoseev et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025]. Both find the fringe visibility set by the overlap of the slit's two momentum states after the photon's kick, \(V=|\langle\psi(p-\hbar k)|\psi(p+\hbar k)\rangle|=e^{-2\eta^2}\), \(\eta=\hbar k/2\Delta p\), tunable through the trap depth (momentum spread from \(0.78\,\hbar k\) to \(1.60\,\hbar k\) in the tweezer experiment), and independent of whether the atom is trapped at all. [C] The visibility is a Gram element of two matter states.

Theorem (the duality relation is the metric–curvature bound)
For a pure which-way marker with states \(|m_+\rangle,|m_-\rangle\), the distinguishability \(D^2\) equals the determinant of the \(2\times2\) Gram matrix of the two marker states, so \(D^2+V^2=1\) is the statement that a PSD Gram determinant is non-negative and vanishes exactly at rank one — the algebra of Theorem 3.1 with the tangent pair \((V,W)\) replaced by the marker pair \((m_+,m_-)\). [T]

Proof. With unit markers, \(G=\begin{pmatrix}1&V\\ \bar V&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(\det G=1-|V|^2\ge0\). The optimal which-way measurement distinguishes the two markers with \(D=\sqrt{1-|V|^2}\) [Englert 1996], so \(D^2=\det G\). Rank one (\(V=1\)) is the one-complex-line case of Theorem 3.1.

Gated (Appendix A.3): on Gaussian markers displaced by \(\pm\hbar k\), \(V\) matches \(e^{-2\eta^2}\) to six figures across \(\eta=0.17\) to \(2.0\), \(D^2+V^2=1\) to \(10^{-16}\), and \(D^2-\det G=0\) to \(10^{-16}\). [V] Light's fringes are read off matter's metric. The ledger — the phase around the two paths — is untouched by the marker; what dies is the pairing, and the which-way information does not vanish with it: for independent scatterers it is robustly stored and recoverable by an eraser, which is the ledger crossing the wall the metric fails on.

4.3 An integer through a wall

Orbital angular momentum is a winding: the topological charge of a beam, \(\hbar\ell\) per photon. In 2025 it was shown to be conserved photon by photon through parametric down-conversion — a single heralded pump photon of charge \(\ell_p\) produces a pair with \(\ell_s+\ell_i=\ell_p\), the pump destroyed inside the crystal and its integer kept, at one conversion in a billion, \(1.3\) heralded pairs per hour over \(168\) hours, with \(99.5\%\) correlation against the classical-pump map [Kopf et al. 2025]. [C] A laser-pumped conversion can only witness the conservation of the average; the single-photon measurement was needed because averages erase integers — the content of Section 7's table.

The anchor of Section 3.3 is the same object in finite dimensions. Gated (Appendix A.4): the pair \((U^k,V)\) in \(d=31\) returns \(w=k\) exactly for \(k=1,2,3,5,10,15\), defect \(\delta_k=4\sin^2(k\pi/d)\) to nine digits, the ratio \(\delta_k/(16k^2/d^2)\) running \(2.459\to1.065\) toward \(1\) so the constant \(\tfrac14\) in the floor \(|w|\le(d/4)\sqrt\delta\) is optimal, and the sharp integer bound \(|w|\le\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor=15\) attained. [V] One loop, one integer; the defect an integer costs is a sine squared; and the exchange rate between a normalized amplitude and an unnormalized integer is exactly one quarter. This is the moduli side of the paper: \(U\mapsto U^k\) pushes a representation off its character variety by a controlled amount, and the integer is what the almost-representation keeps of the exact one.

4.4 The same object at every scale

Time-of-flight limits from tens of thousands of TeV photons of GRB 221009A put any linear energy dependence of the speed of light above \(5.9\) to \(6.2\) Planck energies [Piran–Ofengeim 2024]; a 2025 global analysis in the Standard-Model-Extension language tightens the photon-sector coefficients by an order of magnitude with no deviation [Guerrero et al. 2025]; fast-radio-burst dispersion, with the Hubble function reconstructed without assuming a cosmology, bounds the photon mass below \(2.0\times10^{-15}\) eV/\(c^2\) at one sigma [Ran et al. 2024]. [C] Section 3.5's dictionary has no term for light because there is nothing for a scale to multiply; these are the measurements of that absence. They constrain the linear-dispersion and photon-mass classes they test; they do not fix the form of any remaining Planck-scale correction.


4.5 Light borrows the low rail and returns

Classical Maxwell theory in vacuum is linear, so classical light does not scatter light. Quantum electrodynamics permits it, through a virtual electron–positron loop, and the LHC has now measured it in ultra-peripheral lead collisions: \(78\pm15\) nb [ATLAS 2019] and \(107\pm24\) nb [CMS 2025], against a leading-order prediction near \(70\) nb and next-to-leading-order calculations now carried to three loops [Bargieła et al. 2026]. [C] The box diagram is a round trip: light leaves the ledger, takes a momentary rest-frame census in matter's register, and returns. Maxwell is the low-energy limit of a nonlinear vacuum. That the supply between the rails has a name in the standard theory is a reading, not a derivation of this paper. [H]


4.6 The shape of a photon is the environment's

The exact quantum electrodynamics of an emitter in a lossy radiative environment maps the continuum of modes onto a discrete set of non-Hermitian pseudomodes and computes the emitted photon's profile from them [Yuen–Demetriadou 2024]. [C] The profile is the environment's, not the photon's: light supplies the content and the boundary selects the mode. Section 5.2 measures the same object on a lattice — the dominant pseudomode of a non-normal operator concentrating where the light crowds.


4.7 Weak measurement is the first-order ledger

Average photon trajectories through a double slit are reconstructed by coupling weakly to momentum [Kocsis et al. 2011], and relativistic velocity fields for single photons are built the same way [Foo et al. 2022]. [C] The fringes survive because the pointer's back-action is second order. Section 5.4 proves the algebraic reason: the skew sector's first-order contribution to the trace-log ledger vanishes identically. The trajectories are averages, not paths; they are consistent with the field-first reading and do not require another.


5. The reference: light

5.1 The directional dictionary and capacity

Two directions at angle \(\theta\) are distinct trackable channels at threshold \(\varepsilon\) iff \(\sin^2\theta\ge\varepsilon\). [T] Directions at angle \(\theta\) correspond to rank-one projectors at squared Hilbert–Schmidt distance \(2\sin^2\theta\); \(\delta\)-separated directions need \(\varepsilon=\sin^2\delta\approx\delta^2\); \(\mu\) simultaneous needles need \(\mu\) channels, not \(\log\mu\) bits. Angular depth is exact: \(\Lambda=2\log(1/\sin\delta)\).

The boundary census of a region \(A\) is defined as the number of eigenvalues of the restricted projector \(1_AP1_A\) lying in the transition window \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) — the half-occupied modes that live on the boundary; that it is the boundary count of matter is the model's reading of it. Gated on a gapped two-band Dirac lattice, projector kernel asserted Hermitian and idempotent before every reading (Appendix A.5): sixty-four directions enter a disc, a bounded number of channels survive, and the capacity is the same at \(\mu=40\) and \(\mu=64\) — overflow measured directly. Over the five tested decades the capacity growth is consistent with an approximately logarithmic dependence on depth, \(7,9,11,13,14\) on a disc of radius \(4\) against the aperture value \(\pi R/w=12.6\), and \(11,15,18,20,22\) on a disc of radius \(7\) against \(22.0\) — an aperture-scale ceiling, matched to a few percent on the larger disc and overshot by one channel in ten on the four-site one; the boundary census of the same regions runs \(0,24,40,42,48\) and \(0,44,72,76,88\) over the same decades, several times faster, until the gap cuts it off. [V] The price of a direction is depth, not channel count. The object being priced has a name: a directional support set is the smallest admissible carrier — a set of straight unit segments, one in every allowed direction, together with the projector that has to carry them — and the capacity gate measures how many of its directions a region can keep distinct. Kakeya's set is the same object with the projector removed [Beasley 2026a]. What the gate does not show: that the ceiling is exactly \(\pi R/w\), that the growth is logarithmic beyond the tested range, or anything about a continuum field — it is one lattice, one projector, one definition of a needle.

5.2 Overflow, dichotomy, and a wall born inside

In the constructions studied here, where the boundary census cannot support directional demand, overflow is resolved one of two ways: continued full-dimensional support, or concentration onto a lower stratum. [V] The classical extremal question this echoes — how little room a family of needles covering every direction can occupy — is now settled in three dimensions [Wang–Zahl 2025], work recognized with Hong Wang's 2026 Fields Medal [IMU 2026]; Section 6 takes it up directly.

A crossing supplies its own wall. Gated on the tripartite operator \(H=A+D_0+\varepsilon E\) — lattice Laplacian, a detuning that follows the light, a skew flow along the intensity gradient — with two controls, no needles and no skew sector (Appendix A.6): the dominant pseudomode's weight in the \(3\times3\) crossing cell, \(1.15\%\) of the area, rises from the uniform \(9/784=0.0115\) to \(0.628\) once eight or more needles cross at detuning depth \(g=1\); the normal control reaches \(0.466\), so the skew sector adds a third again (excess \(1.35\)); at shallow detuning the skew sector slightly subtracts (excess \(0.93\)), and over-driving it disperses the mode (excess \(0.15\)). [V] The model grows an interior wall on the gradient the crossing itself supplies; the non-normal share of it is gated by the skew-to-detuning ratio, not by the drive alone. What that wall does to a needle that meets it is a refraction: the earlier transport construction derives, from a variational capacity principle, \(R_1\sin^2\theta_1=R_2\sin^2\theta_2\) across an interface whose directional rigidity changes from \(R_1\) to \(R_2\), with the gradient of an effective inertia tensor \(I_{\rm eff}\) doing the turning [Beasley 2026b]. [C] That the same law governs the self-made wall of this section is a reading, not yet a gate. [H] The loop closes on itself: light sets the intensity, the intensity sets the detuning and the skew flow, and those set where the light can go — transport to stress to geometry to transport. Section 9 says what an apparatus would have to do to see it.

6. Does light exhibit the Kakeya effect?

This is the paper's central question, and it has two halves: whether light does it, and whether the people working on the needle problem are approaching it in a way that would find out.


6.1 The problem, at the scale light lives on

Kakeya asked in 1917 how little area a unit needle needs to turn around; Besicovitch showed it needs none [Besicovitch 1928]. The modern conjecture is that a set containing a unit segment in every direction has full Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension in \(\mathbb R^n\): proved in the plane [Davies 1971], with a one-page quantitative form [Córdoba 1977]; bounded below by \(2.5\) in space [Wolff 1995]; and proved in three dimensions [Wang–Zahl 2025], via a multiscale induction on the sticky and grainy structure a counterexample would be forced to have [Guth 2026]. [C] In four and more dimensions it is open; the best tools there are planebrushes and trilinear estimates [Katz–Zahl 2021]. [C]

The reason the problem matters is light. Fefferman showed that the ideal circular low-pass filter — the sharpest aperture there is — is an unbounded operator on \(L^p\) for \(p\ne2\), and the proof is a Besicovitch set [Fefferman 1971]. The Kakeya problem at resolution \(\delta\) is about tubes, and a tube of width \(\delta\) with a direction is exactly a wave packet of wavelength \(\delta\): the plane wave carries the direction, the wavelength sets the width. So the quantitative Kakeya question — how much can a family of such tubes overlap — is the question of how far superposed wave packets can concentrate, and it sits under the restriction, Bochner–Riesz and local-smoothing conjectures of harmonic analysis. Local smoothing for the wave equation, that a solution cannot hold its energy in a small region for an extended time [Sogge 1991], implies Kakeya, Bochner–Riesz and restriction; it is proved in the plane [Guth–Wang–Zhang 2020] and on compact surfaces [Gao–Liu–Miao–Xi 2023] and open in space. [C]

6.2 Does light do it?

Two things are true at once and they are not in tension. First, light is the physical carrier of the Kakeya constraint: the object the harmonic-analysis proofs are about is a superposition of wave packets, and the theorem that would carry the constraint to light in three dimensions — local smoothing in \(\mathbb R^{3+1}\) — is the open one, while its consequence for tubes is now proved. [C] Second, the effect light exhibits is the constraint, not the compression. A Besicovitch set of measure zero is a set of segments; a set of tubes of width \(\lambda\) in \(\delta\)-separated directions occupies volume bounded below, up to the logarithm the theorem allows, and no arrangement of the corresponding tube supports beats the geometric bound; whether coherent superpositions can realize an analogous concentration is the open local-smoothing question. That is what the two null gates of Section 5.5 say from the operator side — no smooth local mechanism compresses directional coverage — and what the capacity gate of Section 5.1 says in numbers: sixty-four directions enter and a bounded number of channels survive, fixed by the aperture, however deep one looks. [V] A wavelength-scale family of light tubes cannot evade the geometric Kakeya bound: the tube theorem fixes the room such packets can occupy, at any resolution a wavelength can set. Whether a coherent field, where packets interfere as well as overlap, can realize the analogous concentration is exactly the open local-smoothing estimate of Section 6.1. What light can do is overflow, and overflow has exactly the two horns the dichotomy names: full dimension, which is the theorem, or concentration onto a lower stratum, which is a wall. Section 5.2 measures the wall being born inside a crossing. The needles are light; the Kakeya structure in light is the budget on direction and the strata that budget forces, not a vanishing set.


6.3 Are they approaching it the right way?

Yes, and the gates say why. The proofs that work are discrete and combinatorial at scale \(\delta\) — induction on scales, sticky and grainy decompositions, polynomial partitioning — and they had to be, because the null gates show that smooth local geometry cannot do the job and Section 5.1's overflow shows that the counting is what binds. The winning approach is the one this paper's own instrument would have predicted. Where the approaches differ is in what is counted: the literature counts tubes and their incidences; this paper counts channels and the census that supports them, and finds the count fixed by an aperture and the price paid in depth. Nobody in the Kakeya literature counts channels; nobody connects the packing to a boundary census or an entanglement budget. That lane is open, and it is where an operator formulation could contribute rather than re-derive. What it cannot do is replace the theorem: the capacity plateau is a measurement on one lattice, the dichotomy is an input-dependent proposition, and Wang–Zahl is a theorem about every set. The right posture is the one taken here — cite the theorem as input, measure the operator-side consequences, and name the one bridge the field has not built.

The one place the field's route may be taking the long way is the passage to light itself. Kakeya in three dimensions is proved for tubes, but the wave estimate that would make it a statement about light is still open, and it is a harder object because it must control constructive and destructive interference between packets, not just their overlap [Tao 2026]. [C] The operator side has a native handle on exactly that: the phase is the ledger, interference is the ledger read around a loop, and the first-order cancellation of Section 5.4 is a statement about which interference a skew sector can and cannot produce. Whether that handle reaches local smoothing is Open Problem 2.

8. The supply between the rails

8.1 Three ways for admissibility to fail, not two

The metric can vanish (\(\det g=0\), a curve in two parameters); two eigenvalues can coalesce (isolated points); and the licence \(4\det g\ge\Omega^2\) — the positivity of \(Q=g-\tfrac i2\Omega\) itself — can fail strictly before \(\det g\) does. [T] A curve and a point do not generically meet, so the geometric wall is not the exceptional point. In the two-band families examined here the sampled loci are distinct, with the licence boundary lying inside the positive-metric region, and the innermost is the one the recoiling-slit experiments have not yet looked for (Open Problem 1).

[V] Gated to \(2\times10^{-16}\) with two negative controls (Appendix A.9): a generic probe breaks the equality by a wide margin, a generic generator with a matched probe keeps it. A perfect square is a round trip with no preferred direction; an arrow needs the square broken.


8.3 Why self-adjointness forces the metric positive

Writing \(dP_i=X_i+Y_i\) with \(X_i=\Pi\,dP_i\,(I-\Pi)\), self-adjointness forces \(Y_i=X_i^\dagger\), turning the metric into a Gram matrix, PSD by construction. [T] Gated (Appendix A.10): self-adjoint control gives \(\max\|Y-X^\dagger\|=7\times10^{-16}\) and all-positive eigenvalues; breaking Hermiticity gives \(\|Y-X^\dagger\|=2.57\) and mixed-sign eigenvalues. [V]


8.4 The ledger crosses regardless

A rectifiable contour bounded on the resolvent set gives an analytic Riesz projector regardless of Hermiticity or definiteness — standard operator theory [Kato]. [C] On a two-band family whose round-trip form changes sign between \(\gamma=0.48\) and \(0.49\): \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=2.000000000000\) at every sampled point through the wall, and the shifted ledger \(Z=\tfrac1{2\pi i}\oint\log(z+c)\,\mathrm{Tr}(zI-H)^{-1}dz\), with \(c\) chosen so the branch cut lies outside the contour, equals \(\mathrm{Tr}\log(H+cI)\) to ten digits and varies smoothly with no discontinuity (Appendix A.14). [V] Near a genuine exceptional point nothing stays bounded: a fixed-radius contour's resolvent norm grows from \(1.0\) to \(667\) as the point is approached, and a contour with margin \(\eta=2\sqrt\varepsilon\) grows only from \(0.30\) to \(125\) over the same range — both diverge, the scaled margin provably slower (Appendix A.16). [V] The condition is the contour: the ledger survives the metric's failure for as long as a contour can be kept in the resolvent set, and fails only when the spectrum itself is forced through it. The metric wall and the spectral wall are different objects, and the spectral one is the harder obstruction. This carries no implication about any directional-dimension bound.


8.5 Amplitude dies, the count stays

On \(H=\sigma_x+i\gamma\sigma_z\), exceptional at \(\gamma=1\), the normalized biorthogonal overlap \(|\langle L|R\rangle|\) equals half the gap and falls as \(\sqrt{2(1-\gamma)}\) — to \(0.0045\) at \(1-\gamma=10^{-5}\), Petermann factor \(5\times10^4\) — while the Riesz trace of the colliding pair read by a contour is \(2.000000000000\) at every point and the trace-log ledger varies smoothly (Appendix A.15). [V] Two records on one operator: the pairing dies as the square root of the distance to the wall, the count and the phase do not move. This is the third classifier of Section 3.5 measured, and it is the whole thesis in one table.

8.6 The entropy junction

Two independently published programs place a relative entropy exactly where a metric would sit, both at a horizon: the semiclassical Einstein equations follow from relative entropy across a bifurcate Killing horizon [Dorau–Much 2026], and a covariant construction obtains modified Einstein equations from relative entropy between the physical and matter-induced metrics [Bianconi]. A finite-area completion on the Nariai spacetime has addressed the first's normalization [Hartshorn 2026]. [H] If the metric is a protection budget and the horizon is where protection ends, an entropy is what should be found there. This is a forward connection only; nothing in the paper's argument uses it.


9. Experimental consequences

Three things follow that an apparatus can check. First, the warm slit: for a pure atom the duality relation is exact at every \(\eta\), and for an atom at \(0.91\) ground-state occupation this paper computes \(D^2+V^2\) between \(0.81\) and \(0.94\) with the visibility on the Debye–Waller curve rather than \(e^{-2\eta^2}\) (Appendix A.18); a tunable-trap experiment that also tunes the occupation measures that shortfall directly, and any licence effect beyond it lives in the mixed-marker Gram. Second, the capacity of Section 5.1 against structured light: the number of orbital-angular-momentum modes an aperture keeps distinguishable at a fixed crosstalk threshold is the laboratory form of the channel plateau, and the review of structured quantum light lists the tools that measure it [Forbes et al. 2025]. [C] Third, the closed loop of Section 5.2: an optical reservoir coupled to an electronic absorber and re-emitter, with a non-Hermitian bundle selecting which recycled photons return to a productive interaction, is the architecture in which transport sets geometry sets transport; its figure of merit is useful optical output per input joule, its killer comparison is a plain emitter against a conventional recycling cavity against the geometric bundle, and it survives only if the bundle beats the cavity [Beasley 2026c]. [H] None of these is built here; each is stated so that it can be falsified. The two-rail reading itself has a kill condition: across a \(\det g=0\) crossing, \(\det g\) changes sign while \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi\) stays exactly integer and the shifted ledger stays exact; if in any system the ledger is found to degrade where the metric does, the ordering this paper assigns to light and matter is wrong.


10. Open problems


The licence before the metric, for a marker that is not pure. For a pure atomic slit the licence cannot lapse early: Theorem 4.2's Gram is \(2\times2\) and Theorem 3.1 saturates it identically, which is why the 2025 experiments fit one curve. Gated (Appendix A.18): \(D^2+V^2=1.0000\) at every \(\eta\) for the ground state, while for a thermal atom at the reported \(0.91\) ground-state occupation the sum drops to \(0.81\)–\(0.94\) across the tuning range and the visibility follows the Debye–Waller factor — Englert's inequality being strict for a mixed marker. [V]+[C] The open question is whether, for a mixed or higher-rank marker where the Gram is larger than \(2\times2\), the positivity licence on \(Q\) fails before the trace distance saturates, and by an amount Englert's bound does not already fix. [H]

Local smoothing from the ledger. Whether the first-order cancellation of Section 5.4 and the contour ledger give any handle on the wave-equation estimate that would carry Kakeya to light in three dimensions.

Channels against tubes. The capacity plateau is measured on one lattice; a mode-count limit for structured light through an aperture at a fixed crosstalk threshold is the laboratory quantity it should be compared with.

Real-space propagation. Whether any parameter-space cone or licence locus corresponds to a real-space characteristic surface is not addressed here.

Beyond smooth local geometry. Two null gates rule out smooth mechanisms; whether a genuinely non-smooth, adversarial construction can do what smooth local geometry provably cannot remains open on the operator side.

The non-normal share of the interior wall. Section 5.2 finds the skew sector's contribution gated by a ratio; the boundary in the \((\varepsilon,g)\) plane is not mapped.

The general reciprocity mechanism. Needing only the probe's \(B\)-symmetry is more general than a generator condition; its consequences for a nested-wall structure are not worked out.

The tip–tail feedback loop beyond first order. Only the first-order vanishing is proved.

A non-Riemannian colour-space connection. Whether this framework's ledger reproduces the hue shift independently characterized in [Bujack et al. 2025] is carried as a named question, untested. [H]



11. Conclusion

Built from first principles, the same object keeps appearing: a projector-valued operator whose metric protects locally and can fail, and whose ledger survives globally and provably does not fail with it. Held against the evidence on light, every experiment reads one register: a fringe is matter's metric, a conserved charge is light's integer, a click is a pairing at a point, an interference is a phase carried around a loop. Light supplies the ledger its content because light has nothing to lose to a rest frame; matter supplies the metric its content because matter is exactly what a rest frame is for. Light does not compress direction — it cannot — but it pays for every direction in depth, and where the budget runs out it builds a wall. The gap between the two rails is not a defect in the construction. It is, on the evidence assembled here, the supply.

def hvec(qx, qy, dlt):
    return np.array([2*tso*np.sin(qy), 2*tso*np.sin(qx), dlt/2 - 2*t0*(np.cos(qx)+np.cos(qy))])

# SCRIPT: BOUNDARY-GATE-05 (final -- shorter chain, stronger damping, wider
# time window so the eventual decay is visible within the displayed range;
# same construction as -03, just correctly scaled)
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import expm

N = 5
coupling, damping = 1.0, 1.2

def reflecting_H(N, c):
    H = np.zeros((N, N))
    for i in range(N):
        H[i, (i+1)%N] += c; H[(i+1)%N, i] -= c
    return H

def oneway_H(N, c, lam):
    H = -lam*np.eye(N)
    for i in range(N-1):
        H[i+1, i] += c
    return H

Hone = oneway_H(N, coupling, damping)

# SCRIPT: RECIPROCITY-GATE-03 (final, corrected)
# Claim, proved in three lines: if V is B-symmetric (V^T B = B V) then B V is
# a symmetric matrix, so for ANY vectors x, y (in particular any pair of
# right eigenvectors of a B-symmetric generator L), x^T(BV)y = y^T(BV)x
# identically. Applied to eigenvector pairs this is the reciprocity
# obstruction: the round-trip form Q = M_ij M_ji / gap^2 becomes a perfect
# square M_ij^2/gap^2, hence never negative.
import numpy as np

    r = np.random.default_rng(7+seed_offset)
    B = r.normal(size=(n,n)); B = B@B.T + n*np.eye(n)         # SPD symmetric
    A = r.normal(size=(n,n)); A = (A+A.T)/2                    # symmetric
    L = np.linalg.solve(B, A)                                  # L^T B = B L
    S = r.normal(size=(n,n)); S = (S+S.T)/2
    V = np.linalg.solve(B, S)                                  # V^T B = B V, same recipe
    return L, B, V

# Claim: for a genuine orthogonal projector Pi (Pi^2=Pi, Pi Hermitian) and a
# Hermitian tangent variation dPi, writing dPi = X + Y with X=Pi dPi (1-Pi),
# Y=(1-Pi) dPi Pi, self-adjointness forces Y = X^dagger, turning the metric

def random_projector(n, k):
    A = rng.normal(size=(n,k)) + 1j*rng.normal(size=(n,k))

    return Q @ Q.conj().T

# SCRIPT: LAPLACIAN-GATE-01
# Claim from the quadralogy map's bottom-right node: the discrete second-
# difference operator converges strongly to d^2/dtau^2 as the lattice
# spacing shrinks. This is a real, standard, provable claim -- verify it
# rather than either asserting or discarding it.
import numpy as np

def second_diff_error(N, L=1.0):
    delta = L / N
    tau = np.linspace(0, L, N+1)
    psi = np.sin(3*np.pi*tau/L) * np.exp(-tau)   # smooth C-infinity test function
    psi_pp_exact = -( (3*np.pi/L)**2 )*np.sin(3*np.pi*tau/L)*np.exp(-tau) \
                   - 2*(3*np.pi/L)*np.cos(3*np.pi*tau/L)*np.exp(-tau) \
                   + np.sin(3*np.pi*tau/L)*np.exp(-tau)
    # discrete second difference, interior points only
    psi_pp_disc = (psi[2:] - 2*psi[1:-1] + psi[:-2]) / delta**2
    err = psi_pp_disc - psi_pp_exact[1:-1]
    l2 = np.sqrt(np.sum(err**2) * delta)
    return delta, l2

print("Second-difference operator convergence to d^2/dtau^2 (L^2 norm)")
print(f"{'N':>6} {'delta':>10} {'L2 error':>14} {'error/delta^2':>16}")
prev = None
for N in [10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320, 640]:
    delta, l2 = second_diff_error(N)
    ratio = l2/delta**2
    rate = "" if prev is None else f"  (order {np.log2(prev/l2):.3f} per halving)"
    print(f"{N:6d} {delta:10.5f} {l2:14.6e} {ratio:16.4f}{rate}")

# The wall sits between gamma=0.4 and 0.5. Scan through it and show the
# Riesz projector rank (trace) and the Dunford trace-log ledger stay exact
# while the metric's sign flips.
import numpy as np
sx=np.array([[0,1],[1,0]],complex); sy=np.array([[0,-1j],[1j,0]],complex); sz=np.array([[1,0],[0,-1]],complex)

def H_of(kx, ky, m, gamma):
    d = np.array([kx, ky, m+1j*gamma])
    return d[0]*sx+d[1]*sy+d[2]*sz, d

def roundtrip_Q(d, theta):
    import itertools
    H = d[0]*sx+d[1]*sy+d[2]*sz
    ev, R = np.linalg.eig(H)
    evL, Lraw = np.linalg.eig(H.conj().T)
    best = min(((sum(abs(evL[p[i]].conjugate()-ev[i]) for i in range(2)), p)
                for p in itertools.permutations(range(2))), key=lambda x: x[0])
    L = Lraw[:, best[1]].copy()
    for i in range(2):
        s = np.vdot(L[:,i], R[:,i]); L[:,i] /= s.conjugate()
    gap2 = (ev[1]-ev[0])**2
    V = np.cos(theta)*sx + np.sin(theta)*sy
    A = np.vdot(L[:,1], V@R[:,0]); B = np.vdot(L[:,0], V@R[:,1])
    return (A*B/gap2).real

        Pi += Rinv*dz
        Z += np.log(z + SHIFT)*np.trace(Rinv)*dz   # shifted log: branch cut lies outside the contour

# Question: does the duality relation stay exact for a real trapped-atom slit, or does the
# curve depart at its foot? Marker = the atom's motional state rho; the two paths kick it by
# +-hk, so U_+ U_-^dag = exp(2ikx). Visibility V = |Tr(rho exp(2ikx))|, distinguishability
# D = (1/2)||rho_+ - rho_-||_1 (trace distance). Pure ground state: D^2+V^2 = 1 exactly.
# Thermal state with ground-state occupation p0 (USTC axial 0.91): D^2+V^2 < 1, by how much.
import numpy as np
N = 60                                  # truncated oscillator basis
a = np.diag(np.sqrt(np.arange(1, N)), 1); x = (a + a.T)/np.sqrt(2)   # x in units sqrt(hbar/m w)

    # eta = hk/(2 Dp); ground Dp = 1/sqrt(2) in these units, so 2k x-scale: 2k = 2*eta*2*Dp = ...
    # choose k so that pure-ground V = exp(-2 eta^2): V_pure = exp(-(2k)^2 <x^2>/2) with <x^2>=1/2
    k = np.sqrt(2)*eta_pure
    ev, U = np.linalg.eigh(x)
    return U @ np.diag(np.exp(2j*k*ev)) @ U.conj().T

    q = 1 - p0                          # Boltzmann ratio e^{-hw/kT}
    w = p0*q**np.arange(N); w /= w.sum(); return np.diag(w)
def tracedist(A, B):
    return 0.5*np.sum(np.abs(np.linalg.eigvalsh(A - B)))

# --- 5. subspace vs vector persistence (recomputes the SUBSPACE-PERSISTENCE-GATE-01 family)
rng = np.random.default_rng(3); n, k = 8, 3
S = rng.normal(size=(n,n)); S = (S+S.T)/2
Kk = rng.normal(size=(n,n)); Kk = (Kk-Kk.T)/2
def lowest(Hm, k):
    ev, R = np.linalg.eig(Hm); idx = np.argsort(ev.real)[:k]
    Q, _ = np.linalg.qr(R[:, idx]); return R[:, idx], Q
R0, Q0 = lowest(S, k); v0 = R0[:,0]/np.linalg.norm(R0[:,0])
es = np.linspace(0, 4, 81); vp = []; sp = []; angs = []
for e in es:
    R, Q = lowest(S + e*Kk, k)
    vp.append(max(abs(np.vdot(v0, R[:,j]/np.linalg.norm(R[:,j]))) for j in range(k)))
    sv = np.linalg.svd(Q0.conj().T @ Q, compute_uv=False); sp.append(np.mean(sv**2)); angs.append(np.degrees(np.arccos(np.clip(sv,0,1))))
angs = np.array(angs)
fig, (a1, a2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.3))
a1.plot(es, vp, color=CORAL, lw=2.2, label="best single-vector overlap")
a1.plot(es, sp, color=GOLD, lw=2.2, label="subspace persistence  (1/k)Σcos²θᵢ")
a1.set_xlabel("skew deformation ε"); a1.set_ylim(0, 1.05); a1.legend(frameon=False, fontsize=8, loc="lower left")
for i, c in enumerate([TEAL, GOLD, CORAL]): a2.plot(es, angs[:, i], color=c, lw=1.8, label=f"θ{i+1}")
a2.set_xlabel("skew deformation ε"); a2.set_ylabel("principal angle (deg)"); a2.legend(frameon=False, fontsize=8)
a2.set_title("the hierarchy θ₁ ≤ θ₂ ≤ θ₃", fontsize=9)
