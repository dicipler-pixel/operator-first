1    Introduction and the Master Equation
We work in a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space HN . When the finite-rank cutoff is empha-
sized we write the master operator as HN (ε, Λ); for abstract statements we write K. Adjoints are
Hermitian, denoted †.
    Conventional treatments of gravitation take a metric tensor gab as a fundamental background.
Here spacetime geometry is not postulated: the fundamental data is the continuous tracking of
persistent spectral projection bundles Π on a Grassmannian Gr(k, HN ), and classical geometry is
a derived, large-scale description of the tracking dynamics. On HN = Hspace ⊗ Hgauge we introduce

                                  HN (ε, Λ) = ΛI + A + D0 + εE,                                    (1)

with four structurally distinct sectors: the baseline preload ΛI (absolute scalar reference level;
maintains projector-bundle continuity in the absence of load); the transport sector A (gauge flows,
parallel transport); the obstruction sector D0 (constraint geometry, symmetric energy landscapes);
and the non-normal generator εE (transient amplification, pseudospectra, exceptional points). The
four-tier structural lift—microstate deviations → covariant transport → projector subspaces →
persistent geometry—is as in the companion series; the non-normal generator is the sole source of
departure from normality (Appendix B).

4     The Baseline Operator Theorem
Theorem 4.1 (Baseline Operator Theorem). Let K = K0 + ∆K on H = CM , with K0 = −ΛI and
∆K a bounded off-diagonal perturbation, and let Π be a spectral projector of K0 along an isolated
contour Γ. Then: (1) the projector bundles of K0 are invariant under the unperturbed flow; (2) all
non-normality arises from ∆K, since [K, K † ] = [∆K, ∆K † ]; (3) emergent geometry depends only
on the structure of ∆K; (4) Λ fixes the absolute scalar reference level.

Proof. K0 is a real multiple of the identity: it commutes with every operator, its evolution U0 (τ ) =
e−Λτ I conjugates every projector to itself, and all cross terms in [K0 + ∆K, K0† + ∆K † ] vanish.
Properties (3)–(4) follow since the pullback metric (3) is built from the off-diagonal content of ∆K,
while Λ shifts every eigenvalue uniformly.

nothing else.

10       The Cosmological Constant
This paper began as an attack on the cosmological constant problem. We state exactly which parts
of that problem the framework now addresses, at what grade.

10.1      Λ as conserved spectral cargo
Weinberg’s no-go theorem [C] [8] obstructs dynamical adjustment mechanisms that relax the vac-
uum energy to its observed value. The present framework is not an adjustment mechanism: by


                                                                  8
Theorem 8.1 the relaxation is isospectral, so nothing dynamical ever touches Λ. The flow dissipates
exactly the anisotropic, non-normal dressing (Theorem 8.2) and delivers a normal operator; the
uniform reference level −Λ is conserved cargo. The framework therefore explains, at theorem grade,
why the surviving vacuum stress is isotropic—pure λgab with no anisotropic remainder—while the
value of Λ is reclassified as a spectral invariant of the initial data. We state the cost plainly: a
reclassification is not a derivation of the value. The magnitude question is addressed separately
below through the channel count.
Remark 10.1 (The taring interpretation). The origin of this framework was a metrological suspicion:
that the 10122 discrepancy is the reading of an instrument that was never tared. That suspicion
now has formal content at three grades. (i) [T] By Theorem 4.1, every observable built from
commutators, spectral gaps, or off-diagonal data is invariant under K → K + cI: measurements
of differences never see the tare. (ii) [T] The ledger of Theorem 6.1 is exactly a tared reading,
Zλ = M log(η − Λ) + (load terms): gross equals container plus net, and the calibration condition
