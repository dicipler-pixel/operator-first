Abstract

A transported spectral subspace supports two distinct readings: an
intrinsic tangent pairing and a contour-defined spectral record. The
pairing can become degenerate along a parameter family while the
spectral record remains well defined, provided its contour and analytic
branch remain admissible. This paper develops that distinction as an
operator-first framework for investigating light–matter response. It
separates orthogonal-projector geometry from biorthogonal extensions,
and spectral scalars from measurements that retain the orientation of a
subspace relative to a probe.

Three connections are established within explicitly stated
finite-dimensional models. First, a ground-state projector split across
a spatial cut satisfies \(C_A-C_A^2=BB^\dagger\): its
transition-window census is exactly a singular-value count of the
cross-boundary coupling. This gives a rigorous bound on
boundary-filtered directional channels and reproduces the original
lattice census. Second, Schur elimination followed by a Smith/Cayley
transform pulls the boundary’s non-returned response back to the
Hermitian part of the interior operator, without requiring normality.
Third, electric-field-driven projector geometry determines a
transition-resolved optical polarizability through excitation-energy
weights. Below the gap the metric and the symmetric response have
identical real nullspaces; between resonances an exact numerator zero
can cancel the response while the metric stays nonzero.

Directional rigidity is fixed throughout as \(R_\eta=[\sigma_{\min}(A_{\mathrm{intr}})^2+\eta]^{-1}\)
in declared inner products. Its resolvent representation, stability
bound, and the refraction law of a specified scalar transport action are
proved. The independently calculated optical coefficient is not
identified with this rigidity without a constitutive map. The fixed
symmetric-plus-skew family has an even scalar contour ledger to all
orders, while directed boundary response can change at first order.
Permutation holonomies, graph-subspace angles, and compression
identities supply additional tests of information lost by scalar
readings or intermediate reductions.

The assignment of the two registers to light and matter is an
organizing physical interpretation, not a consequence of the finite
algebra alone. Experiments motivate the questions; the computations
reported here are model calculations. Three research extensions are
reserved for future work: a physical identification of transport
rigidity with the optical coefficient, a continuum optical
directional-capacity bound, and a dynamical test of the long-lived
light-source architecture. The accompanying Lean formalization verifies
91 finite theorem declarations; the physical interpretation and
continuum extensions are outside that certification.



The two-register interpretation. The
metric is a local pairing and the ledger is a spectral record defined on
an admissible domain. The assignment to matter and light is [H]; it is
not an assertion that arbitrary optical phase survives every
interface.

1.
Introduction: what is held, what is transported, what is recorded

A relation is more informative than the labels of the objects it
connects. An optical transition depends on an initial state, a final
state, and the interaction that couples them. A transported subspace
depends on an operator and on which spectral cluster can be continued. A
boundary response depends on an interior operator, a division into
interior and boundary, and a specified input and output. An eigenvalue
list does not contain all of these relations.

The operator-first programme begins with a family \(H(q)\) and asks whether a continuation is
admissible. For a chosen spectral cluster, admissibility means that a
contour \(\Gamma\) can be kept in the
resolvent set and that the scalar function used in the functional
calculus is analytic on the relevant domain. The Riesz projector is
\[
\Pi_\Gamma(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma(zI-H(q))^{-1}\,dz.
\tag{1.1}
\] If \(H(q)\) is analytic and
the contour remains admissible, this projector is analytic. Its rank is
locally constant. Individual eigenvectors inside the cluster may become
poorly conditioned or cease to be individually continuable at a
collision, without destroying the projector of the whole isolated
cluster [2]. [C]

The local question is different. For a smooth orthogonal projector
\(P(q)\), its derivative tells us
whether a parameter variation changes the subspace. The pullback of
(0.1) prices that change. A direction can be null because the projector
does not change in that direction, even though the spectrum used to
define it remains separated. The contour question and the metric
question therefore have different hypotheses and different failure
modes.

A scalar spectral ledger is \[
Z_f(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\Gamma
f(z)\operatorname{Tr}(zI-H(q))^{-1}\,dz.
\tag{1.2}
\] For \(f=1\) this counts the
algebraic multiplicity inside the contour. For a specified analytic
logarithm it gives a branch-dependent trace-log. Such a trace-log may
vary smoothly while the integer rank remains fixed. It is not the entire
optical phase, and it does not determine a Berry holonomy or the
orientation of spectral residues relative to a detector.

The physical reading is that matter supplies localized interactions
and persistent records, while light is especially suited to transporting
phase and conserved charges between interactions. These are the paper’s
two rails. [H] They are not two additional fields, and
the assignment is not exclusive: light has quantum-state geometry,
matter supports phase coherence, and measurements can depend on both.
The mathematical contribution is to specify which reading remains valid
under which continuation, and how a probe-dependent response reconnects
them.



The operator-first architecture, with
corrected metric normalization and explicit branch conditions. The Riesz

The exposition follows the original eleven-section structure.

2. The instrument

2.1 Symmetric and skew parts

For a real square matrix \(C\),
write \(C=S+K\) with \[
S=\frac12(C+C^\top),\qquad K=\frac12(C-C^\top).
\] Then \[
[C,C^\top]=-2[S,K].
\tag{2.1}
\] Proof. Expand \((S+K)(S-K)-(S-K)(S+K)\). The two square
terms cancel and the mixed terms give \(-2(SK-KS)\). Thus \(C\) is normal precisely when \(S\) and \(K\) commute. [T]

The raw stress is \[
\Phi(C)=\frac12\|[C,C^\top]\|_F^2=2\|[S,K]\|_F^2.
\tag{2.2}
\] It obeys \(\Phi(aC)=a^4\Phi(C)\) and is not generally
a squared sine. For \(C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\(\Phi=16\). Any bounded angular
interpretation requires a separately specified normalization. The
original unrestricted equality \(\Phi=\sin^2\theta\) is not used in this
edition.

A complex operator has an analogous Hermitian/skew-Hermitian split
using the adjoint. Results that use a transpose symmetry are not
automatically valid after replacing transposes by adjoints. This
distinction is essential to the ledger parity theorem.

2.2 Rigidity is not a
metric eigenvalue

The authoritative definition is \[
\boxed{R_\eta(A_{\mathrm{intr}})
=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}(A_{\mathrm{intr}})^2+\eta}},\qquad \eta>0.
\tag{2.3}
\] Here \(A_{\mathrm{intr}}\) is
the declared transport operator, represented in fixed Hilbert-space
inner products. In a finite domain, \[
\sigma_{\min}(A)=\min_{\|v\|=1}\|Av\|.
\] This definition includes a domain kernel; a rectangular
numerical implementation must not omit zero singular directions merely
because its economy-size SVD returns fewer entries. The examples and
formal matrix statements below may be taken square.

The original alternative \([\lambda_{\min}(Q)+\eta]^{-1}\) is retired
as a rigidity definition. The exact two-band control in Section 3.1
shows why: the complex Gram can have a null eigenvalue even when its
real metric is strictly positive on the real parameter directions.
Equation (2.3) retains the intended diagnostic of weak transport,
without reinterpreting the complex rank of the tangent Gram.

Normalization is part of the definition. Unitary
changes of input and output frames leave singular values unchanged.
Arbitrary rescalings of units or nonorthogonal coordinate changes need
their corresponding inner-product transformation; they are not numerical
invariances of an unweighted matrix SVD. In a dimensionless model one
can use a fixed reference transport scale. A different pointwise
normalization would define a different rigidity field and must not be
introduced during a fit.

2.3
Resolvent representation and stability of rigidity

Proposition 2.1 (regularized weak-transport
response). For a finite operator \(A\), \[
K_\eta=A^\dagger A+\eta I>0,\qquad
\boxed{R_\eta(A)=\|K_\eta^{-1}\|_2}.
\tag{2.4}
\] Consequently \(0<R_\eta(A)\le1/\eta\), with equality
exactly when \(A\) has a nonzero
kernel.

Proof. The eigenvalues of \(K_\eta\) are \(\sigma_j(A)^2+\eta\). Its inverse is
positive Hermitian, so its operator norm is the reciprocal of the
smallest one. [T]

For two operators between the same spaces, \[
\boxed{|R_\eta(A)-R_\eta(B)|
\le\frac{(\|A\|_2+\|B\|_2)\|A-B\|_2}{\eta^2}}.
\tag{2.5}
\] Indeed, \[
K_A^{-1}-K_B^{-1}
=K_A^{-1}(B^\dagger B-A^\dagger A)K_B^{-1},
\] while \(\|K_A^{-1}\|_2,\|K_B^{-1}\|_2\le1/\eta\)
and \[
B^\dagger B-A^\dagger A=B^\dagger(B-A)+(B^\dagger-A^\dagger)A.
\] The reverse triangle inequality for operator norms completes
the proof. [T] The bound becomes poorly conditioned as
the regularizer is removed; that dependence is expected, not
suppressed.

If a family satisfies \(\sigma_{\min}(A(s))\sim a|s|^p\), with
\(a>0\) and \(p>0\), then \[
R_\eta(s)=\frac{1}{a^2|s|^{2p}[1+o(1)]+\eta}.
\tag{2.6}
\] At fixed \(\eta\) it
saturates at \(1/\eta\). An
unregularized power-law divergence belongs to the regime \(\eta\ll a^2|s|^{2p}\) or to a stated joint
limit. The singular-value exponent must be derived for the actual
operator. A refraction formula does not independently prove that
exponent.

3. The ground: projector
geometry

3.1 The tangent
constraint, metric, and curvature

Let \(P=P^\dagger=P^2\) and let
\(V=V^\dagger\) be a derivative of a
smooth curve of such projectors. Differentiating \(P^2=P\) gives \[
V=PV+VP,\qquad PVP=0,
\] and therefore \[
V=PV(I-P)+(I-P)VP.
\tag{3.1}
\] Thus the tangent is off-diagonal between the occupied subspace
and its complement. Put \(X_V=(I-P)VP\). Then \[
Q_P(V,W)=\operatorname{Tr}(X_V^\dagger X_W),\qquad
Q_P(V,V)=\|X_V\|_F^2.
\tag{3.2}
\] It is a Gram pairing. For two real parameter tangents \(V,W\), positivity of its \(2\times2\) Gram gives \[
\boxed{|\Omega_P(V,W)|\le
2\sqrt{g_P(V,V)g_P(W,W)-g_P(V,W)^2}}.
\tag{3.3}
\] Proof. The determinant is \(g(V,V)g(W,W)-g(V,W)^2-\Omega(V,W)^2/4\ge0\).
Equality means that the off-diagonal maps \(X_V,X_W\) are linearly dependent over the
complex numbers, including a zero map. [T]

For a Hermitian two-band family \(h(\mathbf
k)=\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) with \(\mathbf d\ne0\), \[
P_\pm=\frac12(I\pm\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma).
\] A rank-one band has a one-dimensional complex complement, so
(3.3) is saturated for every tangent pair. This does not mean that every
real tangent direction is null.

The decisive control is \[
P=\frac{I+\sigma_z}{2},\quad V=\frac{\sigma_x}{2},\quad
W=\frac{\sigma_y}{2},
\] for which \[
Q=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\operatorname{spec}Q=\{0,\tfrac12\},\qquad
g=\frac14I,\quad\det g=\frac1{16}.
\tag{3.4}
\] The full complex Gram is singular while the real two-direction
metric is regular. This is the exact reason for keeping the rigidity
definition (2.3) separate.

3.2 Redistribution and
the interaction probe

For an orthogonal projector \(P\)
and Hermitian generator \(D\), define
\[
F(D,P)=(I-P)DP+PD(I-P).
\tag{3.5}
\] It is Hermitian, traceless, and off-diagonal. In the block
decomposition given by \(P\), it
vanishes exactly when \([D,P]=0\).
Equivalently \(F=[P,[P,D]]\).
[T]

In orthonormal occupied and unoccupied bases, \[
\boxed{\frac12\|F(D,P)\|_F^2
=\sum_{n\in\mathrm{occ},\,m\in\mathrm{unocc}}
|\langle m|D|n\rangle|^2}.
\tag{3.6}
\] The two off-diagonal blocks are adjoints and have equal
Frobenius norm, which proves the formula. It is an unweighted
transition-strength sum. An absorption spectrum additionally needs
energies, occupations, frequency dependence, and a broadening
prescription. Section 4 derives that weighting for a finite ground-state
model.

3.3 The noncommutative anchor

Let \(U,V\in U(d)\) and let \(\mathcal U=UVU^\dagger V^\dagger\). Define
\[
\delta=\frac1d\|\mathcal U-I\|_F^2,\quad
g_{\mathrm{br}}=\min_j|\lambda_j(\mathcal U)+1|,\quad
\mathfrak w=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_j\operatorname{Arg}\lambda_j(\mathcal U).
\] Proposition 3.1. If \(g_{\mathrm{br}}>0\), then \(\mathfrak w\in\mathbb Z\), \(|\mathfrak w|\le\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\),
and \[
\boxed{\delta+g_{\mathrm{br}}^2\le4}.
\tag{3.7}
\] The integer is unchanged along a continuous commutator family
for which the branch guard stays positive. [T]

Proof. The determinant of a multiplicative
commutator is one. Thus the sum of the principal eigenphases is an
integer multiple of \(2\pi\). Each
phase lies strictly between \(-\pi\)
and \(\pi\), giving the integer bound.
Continuity and integer-valuedness give local constancy. For each unit
eigenvalue, \(|\lambda-1|^2+|\lambda+1|^2=4\). Average
the first term and bound the second below by its minimum. Equality in
(3.7) holds exactly when all eigenvalues have the same real part.

Also \(|\theta|\le(\pi/2)|e^{i\theta}-1|\) for
\(|\theta|\le\pi\). Cauchy–Schwarz
therefore gives \[
|\mathfrak w|\le\frac d4\sqrt\delta.
\tag{3.8}
\] Clock and shift attain \(\delta+g_{\mathrm{br}}^2=4\), with the
principal branch excluded at \(d=2\).
Their higher winding examples approach the sharp constant \(1/4\) in (3.8). The source’s determinant is
always one; the branch-resolved phase sum retains information that this
determinant alone discards. This is the familiar obstruction setting of
almost-commuting unitaries [3], not an identification with every
conserved optical charge.



The admissible domain in its actual
coordinates: the boundary is \(\delta=4-g_{\mathrm{br}}^2\), not a
quarter-circle. Clock-and-shift examples lie on it. The excluded
two-dimensional example is at \((4,0)\). This replaces the original
schematic axis placement.

3.4 A
realization calibrated against published data

Yi and collaborators extract the quantum geometric tensor of an
optical Raman lattice by Bloch-state tomography [4]. The supplied
calibration script uses its stated two-band tight-binding parameters and
reports \(C=-0.99931\) against the
manuscript’s cited experimental value \(-1.00\pm0.02\). Its finite-difference
metric–curvature ratio has mean \(0.999958\), with minimum \(0.818160\) near poorly resolved small
quantities, and its reported quantum volume is \(1.1454\). The exact pointwise identity is
(3.3); the finite-grid ratios are numerical diagnostics, not a
replacement for it. [C]+[V]

The calibration script was rerun for this edition. It is a
calculation based on a published model and measurement, not laboratory
data produced by this programme. The source scripts and their parameter
values are preserved so that a changed discretization or sign convention
can be compared rather than silently substituted.

3.5
Matter’s boundary count and a conditional scale dictionary

The boundary census is defined operationally in Section 5.1 from a
restricted orthogonal projector. Interpreting it as a count of matter’s
active boundary degrees of freedom is a model reading. Area-law
intuition does not make every substance a gapped state, and condensation
is not universally equivalent to opening a gap. For free fermions,
logarithmically enhanced entropy at a Fermi surface has a precise
literature [5]; translating entropy bounds into a particular threshold
count requires the count’s definition and assumptions.

The earlier conditional scale argument is retained in its actual
scope. With \(\hbar,c,\alpha\) held
fixed, \[
a_0=\frac{\hbar}{\alpha mc}
\] forces a proposed scaling \(a_0\propto\ell\) to have \(m\propto\ell^{-1}\). If an energy scale is
\(mc^2\) and time is defined through
\(E=\hbar/t\), then \(t\propto\ell\). Under the additional
assumption \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\propto
m\), the relation \(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2=\hbar c/G\) gives \(G\propto\ell^2\) and invariant \(Gm^2/(\hbar c)\). These are algebraic
consequences of the premises. [T] No cosmological
scaling, variable fundamental constant, or photon dispersion law is
inferred from them. None of the constitutive results below depends on
these extra premises.

3.6 What the
discrete continuum check establishes

For a sufficiently smooth periodic scalar field, the centered
difference \[
\Delta_h\psi(x)=\frac{\psi(x+h)-2\psi(x)+\psi(x-h)}{h^2}
\] has \(L^2\) consistency error
\(O(h^2)\), for example with \(\psi\in H^4\) and the usual
translation-based comparison. Taylor’s formula with integral remainder
or Fourier multipliers gives a bound proportional to \(h^2\|\psi^{(4)}\|_2\). [T]
The source sine-wave gate approaches second-order convergence as the
mesh is refined. [V]

This proves consistency of this derivative on this regularity class.
Spectral projectors, threshold counts, singular strata, nonlinear
feedback, and wave-packet concentration each require additional
stability or convergence estimates. They do not inherit a continuum
theorem merely because the second difference is consistent.

3.7
The subspace is the vehicle; orthogonal range projectors grade it

Let \(Q_0,Q\) be orthonormal frames
for two rank-\(k\) subspaces, and let
\(P_0^o=Q_0Q_0^\dagger\), \(P^o=QQ^\dagger\). Their principal angles
satisfy \[
\boxed{\frac1k\operatorname{Tr}(P_0^oP^o)
=\frac1k\|Q_0^\dagger Q\|_F^2
=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k\cos^2\theta_j}.
\tag{3.9}
\] This follows by taking the singular values of the
frame-overlap matrix. [T] For \(k=1\) it is a ray overlap. For \(k>1\), a selected vector may rotate
substantially within a subspace whose range barely changes.

For a nonnormal spectral family, the Riesz projection \(\Pi_\Gamma\) need not be orthogonal.
Equation (3.9) applies to orthogonal projections onto its ranges, not in
general to \(\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_{\Gamma,0}\Pi_\Gamma)\).
The exact idempotents \[
P_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
P_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
\] have \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_0P_1)=2\), outside the
rank-one principal-angle range.

The source QR-based range calculation is retained with this
distinction. It tracks the three lowest-real-part modes and reports
larger range persistence than its selected single-vector overlap.
Sorting by real part is not itself an admissible spectral-contour
continuation across a collision with a fourth mode. The graph is a range
diagnostic of that selection, not a theorem that the same isolated
cluster crosses every collision.



Source range-persistence diagnostic under
a skew deformation. The subspace curve uses orthonormalized ranges.
Around a collision with an excluded eigenvalue, the sorted-mode
selection must not be called an automatically admissible rank-three
Riesz continuation.

4. What light is, on the
evidence

The historical wave–particle discussion becomes more useful when
translated into specified measurements. A click is a recorded
interaction; an interference pattern depends on coherence between
alternatives; an optical spectrum depends on the relation between
material states and an electromagnetic probe. These descriptions
overlap. The experiments in this section motivate the two-register
interpretation, but do not uniquely establish it against all other
formulations of quantum theory.



An evidence map, not an exclusivity
theorem. Localization constraints, coherence, angular-momentum
correlations, and light-by-light scattering probe different aspects of
quantum light. The arrows denote an interpretive comparison, not a
sequence through an on-shell electron–positron pair. Numerical limits
depend on the models and confidence conventions used by their
sources.

4.1 No rest frame
does not mean no state geometry

A photon has no inertial rest frame. Photon localization also has
special constraints: the Newton–Wigner construction does not provide the
ordinary massive-particle position observable for a photon under its
localization assumptions [6]. Nevertheless, position-representation
electromagnetic wavefunctions, local fields, and detection probabilities
have rigorous formulations; the relation between the Bialynicki-Birula
and Landau–Peierls constructions is studied in [7].
[C]

These facts do not imply that photons lack a metric on their quantum
states, or that an absence of rest frame proves a contour ledger’s
stability. The intrinsic metric in this paper lives on a family of
subspaces, not on a preassigned particle position. The ledger needs an
admissible resolvent contour, not a rest-frame hypothesis. Keeping those
domains separate is essential to the two-register interpretation rather
than an exception to it.

4.2
Complementarity and two different Gram matrices

The 2025 single-atom experiments of Fedoseev and collaborators and
Zhang and collaborators realize complementary versions of
recoil-sensitive light scattering [8,9]. They support describing fringe
coherence through the overlap of matter states after alternative kicks.
Trapping, wave-packet spread, and marker mixedness must be specified;
there is not one universal visibility formula for every atomic state.
[C]

Proposition 4.1 (pure balanced marker). For
normalized marker states \(|m_+\rangle,|m_-\rangle\), set \(v=\langle m_+|m_-\rangle\) and \(V=|v|\). With equal path priors and optimal
discrimination, \[
G=\begin{pmatrix}1&v\\\bar v&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
D^2=\det G=1-V^2,\qquad D^2+V^2=1.
\tag{4.1}
\] Proof. The density-matrix difference of the
two pure states is supported on their span and has nonzero eigenvalues
\(\pm\sqrt{1-|v|^2}\). Its half trace
norm is therefore \(D=\sqrt{1-|v|^2}\).
Direct evaluation of the Gram determinant proves the rest.
[T] The mixed-marker visibility/distinguishability
inequality is discussed by Englert [10].

The marker Gram in (4.1) has rank two whenever \(D>0\). It has rank one only when \(V=1\). By contrast, the tangent Gram in
Section 3.1 can have complex rank one throughout a two-band family while
its real metric has rank two. The common tool is Gram positivity; these
are not the same Gram or the same rank condition.



The Gaussian pure-marker control, with

Proposition 4.2 (mixed-marker positivity at every

\tag{4.2}
\] Thus mixedness can change visibility, distinguishability, and

4.3 An integer through an
interaction

Kopf and collaborators study orbital-angular-momentum conservation in
spontaneous parametric down-conversion driven by single photons [11]. In
the selected angular-momentum modes the correlations obey the
pump–signal–idler conservation rule. This supplies an example of a
conserved relation across an interaction that does not preserve the
incoming photon as an individual object. [C]

An ensemble average and a single-photon coincidence measurement
answer different statistical questions. It is too strong to say that all
averages erase integers, or that the finite commutator winding in
Section 3.3 is the same observable as optical orbital angular momentum.
Their shared lesson is that a normalized amplitude reading and a
conserved charge need not contain the same information.



Selected orbital-angular-momentum
channels in a single-photon-pumped down-conversion process [11]. The
illustrated charges sum to the pump charge. This is experimental
motivation for tracking relations across an interaction, not a proof
that every contour winding equals an optical charge.

4.4 Limits on dispersion and
mass

Time-of-flight studies of GRB 221009A constrain specified
Lorentz-invariance-violating dispersion models. Piran and Ofengeim
report lower linear-effect scales of approximately \(5.9\) and \(6.2\) Planck energies for their subluminal
and superluminal analyses [12]. Those numbers are parameter bounds, not
observations of photons with Planck-scale energies. Fast-radio-burst
analyses also bound photon mass within their propagation and statistical
models [13]. [C]

Such results constrain physical models of propagation. They do not
prove exact scale independence of every conceivable correction, and do
not identify a spectral ledger with the electromagnetic field. This
edition uses them as context, not as premises in a mathematical
theorem.

4.5 Light scattering from
light

Classical Maxwell theory in vacuum is linear. Quantum field theory
permits photon–photon scattering through virtual charged contributions.
ATLAS and CMS have measured light-by-light scattering in ultraperipheral
heavy-ion collisions [14,15]. Their fiducial selections and
uncertainties must be retained when numerical cross sections are
compared. [C]

A virtual electron loop is not a period in which a photon acquires a
literal rest frame or occupies a classical material census. The phrase
“light borrows the low rail and returns” is an interpretation of an
interaction diagram. [H] It is not used to derive the
electronic response formula below, which has its own Hamiltonian and
probe.

4.6 The
environment and the measured photon profile

An emitter’s electromagnetic environment changes its available modes
and couplings. Yuen and Demetriadou provide a pseudomode quantization
for radiative photonic environments [16]. [C] This
supports studying the coupled emitter–environment response rather than
assigning an emitted wave packet’s shape to an isolated carrier alone.
It does not establish that every pseudomode of the finite crossing model
in Section 5 is a physical optical mode.

4.7
Weak measurement and scalar parity are different claims

Weak-measurement reconstructions of average photon flow, including
the two-slit measurements of Kocsis and collaborators and subsequent
relativistic formulations, provide useful operational examples [17,18].
[C] They do not make the fixed skew-family trace-log

4.8
From an electric-field-driven projector to polarizability

Electronic energies alone do not specify optical coupling. The
interaction matrix elements are also required, as emphasized in
quantum-geometric optical-response theory [19,20]. The following finite
construction supplies the precise parameter and probe needed to connect
the intrinsic metric to a material response.

Let \(H_0\) be Hermitian with a
nondegenerate ground state \(|0\rangle\), excited states \(|m\rangle\), and positive gaps \(\Delta_m=E_m-E_0\). Let \(D_i\) be Hermitian electric-dipole
operators and specify \[
H(\mathcal E)=H_0-\sum_i\mathcal E_iD_i.
\tag{4.3}
\] All derivatives below are taken at zero applied electric
field. Put \(a_{mi}=\langle
m|D_i|0\rangle\). Differentiating the eigenvalue equation and
projecting onto \(m>0\) gives \[
\langle m|\partial_i0\rangle=\frac{a_{mi}}{\Delta_m}.
\] The gauge-dependent component along \(|0\rangle\) disappears from the projector
derivative, so \[
Q_{ij}=\sum_{m>0}\frac{\overline{a_{mi}}a_{mj}}{\Delta_m^2},\qquad
g_{ij}=\sum_m g^{(m)}_{ij},\quad
g^{(m)}_{ij}=\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\overline{a_{mi}}a_{mj})}{\Delta_m^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\] Each \(g^{(m)}\) is positive
semidefinite on real field directions because \(e^\top g^{(m)}e=|\sum_i
a_{mi}e_i|^2/\Delta_m^2\).

For time dependence \(e^{-i\omega
t}\), photon energy \(x=\hbar\omega\), ground-state occupation,
and lossless weak-field response, the linearized density matrix
satisfies \[
[H_0,\delta\rho_j]-x\delta\rho_j=[D_j,P].
\] Its driven matrix elements are \[
(\delta\rho_j)_{m0}=\frac{a_{mj}}{\Delta_m-x},\qquad
(\delta\rho_j)_{0m}=\frac{\overline{a_{mj}}}{\Delta_m+x}.
\] At zero frequency the adiabatic derivative is chosen with no
first-order population change. Undriven resonances elsewhere in the
Liouvillian are handled by continuation of this driven response. Reading
the dipole yields \[
\alpha_{ij}(x)=\sum_m\left[
\frac{\overline{a_{mi}}a_{mj}}{\Delta_m-x}
+\frac{a_{mi}\overline{a_{mj}}}{\Delta_m+x}
\right].
\tag{4.5}
\]

Theorem 4.3 (transition-resolved constitutive
weighting). Away from the transition poles, the
transpose-symmetric part of the lossless response is \[
\boxed{\alpha_s(x)=\sum_m
\frac{2\Delta_m^3}{\Delta_m^2-x^2}\,g^{(m)}}.
\tag{4.6}
\] Proof. Symmetrizing (4.5) replaces each
transition numerator by its real part. The sum of its two denominators
is \(2\Delta_m/(\Delta_m^2-x^2)\), and
the real numerator is \(\Delta_m^2g^{(m)}\). Multiply and sum.
[T]

In particular \(\alpha(0)=2\sum_m\Delta_mg^{(m)}\). For one
excitation gap, \[
\alpha_s(x)=\frac{2\Delta^3}{\Delta^2-x^2}\,g.
\tag{4.7}
\] The excitation-energy weights are essential. A total metric
alone does not in general determine the frequency-dependent
polarizability. The full tensor can also have an antisymmetric,
gyrotropic part, which has not been replaced by the real metric.

4.9
The same silent directions below the gap, and a different zero between
poles

Corollary 4.4 (subgap nullspaces). If \(|x|<\min_m\Delta_m\), write \(w_m=2\Delta_m^3/(\Delta_m^2-x^2)>0\).
Then \[
w_{\min}g\preceq\alpha_s(x)\preceq w_{\max}g,
\qquad
\boxed{\ker\alpha_s(x)=\ker g}.
\tag{4.8}
\] Proof. Each quadratic form is a sum of the
same nonnegative squared transition amplitudes, with strictly positive
weights. A sum vanishes precisely when every contributing amplitude
vanishes. Equivalently, stack real and imaginary amplitudes into \(J\) to obtain \(g=J^\top J\) and \(\alpha_s=J^\top WJ\) with \(W>0\); both kernels are \(\ker J\). [T]

These are directions in the applied electric-field parameter, not
automatically spatial wavevector directions or aperture modes. Equal
rank does not imply equal thresholded capacity: nonzero response
magnitudes change under reweighting.

The positive-weight hypothesis has an exact negative control. Let
\[
H_0=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,2),\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&0&0\\2&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\] Then \(g_{\mathcal E\mathcal
E}=2\), while, with \(y=x^2\),
\[
\alpha(y)=\frac2{1-y}+\frac{16}{4-y}
=\frac{24-18y}{(1-y)(4-y)}.
\tag{4.9}
\] At \(y=4/3\), \(\alpha=0\) although \(g=2\) and neither denominator vanishes. The
two transitions cancel. [T] This is a response zero,
not a metric null or a gap closing.

Proposition 4.5 (unique two-pole tune-out). For
squared-gap poles \(a<b\) and
residues \(A,B>0\), the lossless
scalar response \[
f(y)=\frac{A}{a-y}+\frac{B}{b-y}
\] has exactly one zero away from the poles: \[
y_* = \frac{Ab+Ba}{A+B},\qquad a<y_*<b.
\] Indeed, its numerator is \(Ab+Ba-(A+B)y\), and positive residues place
its root strictly between the poles. The example (4.9) is the case \((a,b,A,B)=(1,4,2,16)\). This theorem
concerns the stated two-pole rational model, without linewidth.
[T]

Exact metric-only control. In the scalar transition
model \(g=d^2/\Delta^2\) and \(\alpha(0)=2d^2/\Delta\), the choices \((d,\Delta)=(1,1)\) and \((2,2)\) have the same \(g=1\) but static responses two and four.
Even static response therefore needs excitation-energy information in
addition to the total field metric. [T]

A prescribed positive linewidth changes the model. Replacing \(x\) by \(x+i\gamma\) gives, for one real
polarization and \(x,\gamma>0\),
\[
\operatorname{Im}\alpha(x+i\gamma)
=\sum_m\frac{4\Delta_m^3x\gamma\,g^{(m)}(e,e)}
{(\Delta_m^2-x^2+\gamma^2)^2+4x^2\gamma^2}\ge0.
\] An exact zero of the complex response is then generally
removed. This linewidth is phenomenological; it is not a microscopic
derivation of dissipation.

4.9a Two
frequencies recover the silent subspace

A zero in one reading need not be a missing coupling. The useful
question is what second reading forces the distinction. The
exact-closure approach used in the arithmetic Kakeya investigation
suggests retaining both sides of that question: a certificate when the
readings determine the target, and an explicit obstruction when they do
not. The optical consequence below follows from the Light model itself;
it does not import a Kakeya construction into a material.

Assumptions. Retain the finite, ground-state,
weak-field, lossless model of Section 4.8, with fixed positive gaps and
fixed dipole matrix elements. The field direction \(e\) is real. Write \(y=x^2\), \(p_m=\Delta_m^2\), \(c_m=2\Delta_m^3>0\), and \(R(y)=\alpha_s(\sqrt y)\) for \(y\ge0\). Choose \(y_1<y_2\) with no transition pole in the
closed interval \([y_1,y_2]\). No
linewidth, frequency-dependent transition numerator, or change of state
is included.

Proposition 4.5a (positive response difference).
Define \[
\beta_m=\frac{c_m}{(p_m-y_1)(p_m-y_2)}>0,
\qquad
B_{12}=\frac{R(y_2)-R(y_1)}{y_2-y_1}.
\] Then \[
B_{12}=\sum_m\beta_m g^{(m)},\qquad
\beta_{\min}g\preceq B_{12}\preceq\beta_{\max}g,
\qquad
\ker B_{12}=\ker g.
\] The extrema are over the finite transition list, assumed
nonempty. In particular, \[
e^\top R(y_1)e=e^\top R(y_2)e=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e\in\ker g,
\qquad
\ker R(y_1)\cap\ker R(y_2)=\ker g.
\] Proof. Subtract each rational weight: \[
\frac{1}{p-y_2}-\frac{1}{p-y_1}
=\frac{y_2-y_1}{(p-y_1)(p-y_2)}.
\] The two factors in the denominator have the same sign because
no pole is crossed. Each \(g^{(m)}\) is
positive semidefinite, and their sum is \(g\), giving the bounds. A positive weighted
sum has zero quadratic form precisely when every summand does; for
positive semidefinite matrices this is equivalent to membership in every
summand’s kernel. If both scalar responses vanish, their difference
vanishes and therefore \(ge=0\).
Conversely \(ge=0\) kills each
transition contribution at every non-pole frequency. The matrix-kernel
intersection follows by the same argument. [T]

Thus even between resonances, where a single response may cancel, its
divided difference retains precisely the metric’s silent subspace. In
fact equal scalar responses at two such frequencies already imply that
both are zero. For a coupled direction the scalar response is strictly
increasing in \(y\) on each pole-free
interval. There is at most one tune-out in that interval. This
generalizes the uniqueness aspect of Proposition 4.5 to any finite
number of positive-residue transitions; it does not assert a zero exists
in every interval for every direction.

Quantitative reading with declared errors. Let
measured scalar readings be \(z_i\)
with deterministic error bounds \(|z_i-e^\top
R(y_i)e|\le\epsilon_i\). Then \[
0\le e^\top g e\le
\frac{z_2-z_1+\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2}
{(y_2-y_1)\beta_{\min}}.
\] A negative numerator is inconsistent with the model and the
declared error bounds. If \(|z_i|\le\tau_i\), a conservative upper
bound replaces that numerator by \(\tau_1+\tau_2+\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\).
Exact darkness requires exact zeros; finite precision certifies an upper
bound on coupling. The constant requires transition-gap information or a
justified positive lower bound. This is an error propagation statement,
not an instrument specification. [T]

Checks and failure modes. In the paper’s example,
\(R(y)=2/(1-y)+16/(4-y)\) and \(g=2\). The tune-out is \(y_1=4/3\); a second reading at \(y_2=2\) gives \(R(y_2)=6\). Both points lie between the
same two poles. The original dark-direction ambiguity is resolved
without measuring either transition separately. [V] If
a pole is crossed, the sign proof fails. For instance \(1/(1-y)+1/(4-y)+1/(9-y)\) has two zeros
\(7/3\) and \(7\) in different intervals despite nonzero
coupling. Signed residues also invalidate the result: \(1/(1-y)-(8/5)/(4-y)+(63/5)/(9-y)\) vanishes
at both \(y=2\) and \(y=3\) in one pole-free interval. These are
exact negative controls. [T]

4.9b What a
collection of probes actually determines

For a fixed real symmetric response \(R\in\mathrm{Sym}_d\), a real probe \(u\) reads \(q_R(u)=u^\top
Ru=\operatorname{Tr}(Ruu^\top)\). Consider known probes \(u_i\) and a desired linear observable \(\operatorname{Tr}(RF)\) with \(F\) real symmetric.

Proposition 4.5b (probe certificate or invisible
perturbation). The known readings determine the target for
every real symmetric \(R\) if and only
if \[
F\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{u_i u_i^\top\}.
\] A certificate \(F=\sum_i a_i u_i
u_i^\top\) gives the exact reconstruction \(\operatorname{Tr}(RF)=\sum_i a_i
q_R(u_i)\). Otherwise there exists a symmetric \(H\) with \[
u_i^\top H u_i=0\quad\text{for every }i,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(HF)\ne0.
\] Proof. The forward reconstruction follows by
linearity. If \(F\) is outside the
span, its nonzero orthogonal component in the Frobenius inner product
supplies \(H\): its inner product with
\(F\) is its squared norm. Then \(R\) and \(R+tH\) have identical known readings but
different targets for \(t\ne0\). Taking
\(R=cI\) with \(c>|t|\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) makes both
matrices positive definite, so ambiguity also occurs within that class.
This is a uniform identifiability statement. Special observed values,
such as exact zero readings on a positive semidefinite boundary, can
imply extra information. Additional structural constraints need a
separate analysis. [T]

For rational probes and a rational target, both alternatives admit
rational certificates and exact verification. An implementation
vectorizes the upper triangle: diagonal coefficients are \(u_i^2\) and off-diagonal coefficients are
\(2u_i u_j\). The factor two matters.
Standard row-space duality supplies the method; no priority claim is
made for that linear algebra.

For example, the two axial probes in dimension two leave \(H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
invisible. Adding the unnormalized probe \((1,1)\) gives \[
R_{12}=\tfrac12\{q_R(1,1)-q_R(1,0)-q_R(0,1)\}.
\] The axial probes and every pairwise sum reconstruct a general
real symmetric \(d\)-dimensional
response using \(d(d+1)/2\) scalar
readings. With reading error bounds \(\epsilon_i\), a displayed certificate has
target error at most \(\sum_i|a_i|\epsilon_i\). Full complex
Hermitian response additionally requires probes that resolve imaginary
cross components. [T]

The gain for the programme is operational: retain the orientation of
the coupling, identify the readings that close the question, and expose
what remains invisible. The arithmetic search and the optical
reconstruction share this exact linear-algebra discipline. They do not
thereby establish a physical identification between their objects.

5.1
Directional resolution is not automatically a channel rank

Two normalized feature rays represented by rank-one orthogonal
projectors obey \[
\frac12\|P_u-P_v\|_F^2=1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2=\sin^2\theta.
\tag{5.1}
\] Thus the chosen pairwise distinction threshold is \(\sin^2\theta\ge\varepsilon\). For an
angular separation \(\delta\), this
gives \(\varepsilon=\sin^2\delta\) and
angular depth \(\Lambda=-\log\varepsilon=2\log(1/\sin\delta)\).
[T] This is a definition of resolution in the feature
space, not a universal detector law.

Pairwise separation does not guarantee independent channels. For
example the three unit vectors \((1,0)\), \((3/5,4/5)\), and \((3/5,-4/5)\) are separated by squared sines
at least \(16/25\), yet their linear
span has dimension two. A multi-input capacity therefore needs its own
Gram or singular-value definition, input normalization, and
threshold.

The source lattice uses projected, restricted, normalized Gaussian
needles as the columns of \(\Phi\) and
counts eigenvalues of \(\Phi^\dagger\Phi\) above a threshold. On
its finite periodic two-band lattice, this is a well-defined collective
response reading. An absolute Gram threshold can depend on how the input
dictionary is sampled or duplicated. It must not be treated as an
invariant of the aperture without a continuum and input-measure
specification.

The exact cross-boundary
census

Let a finite orthogonal ground-state projector split across region
\(A\) and its complement as \[
P=\begin{pmatrix}C_A&B\\B^\dagger&C_{\bar A}\end{pmatrix}.
\] Theorem 5.1 (coupling census). For \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), \[
\boxed{C_A-C_A^2=BB^\dagger},
\tag{5.2}
\] \[
\boxed{N_\varepsilon(A)
=\#\{j:s_j(B)^2\ge\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\}},
\qquad
N_\varepsilon(A)\le\frac{\|B\|_F^2}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}.
\tag{5.3}
\] Here \(N_\varepsilon(A)\)
counts the eigenvalues of \(C_A\) in
\([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\),
including multiplicity.

Proof. The upper-left block of \(P^2=P\) gives (5.2). Since \(0\le C_A\le I\), its eigenvalues are in
\([0,1]\), and those of \(BB^\dagger\) are \(\nu_j(1-\nu_j)\). The factorization \[
\nu(1-\nu)-\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)
=(\nu-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\nu)
\] shows that this is nonnegative exactly on the stated
transition window. Any surplus zero eigenvalues in a rectangular
singular-value convention are below the positive threshold. Summing the
minimum contribution of every counted eigenvalue proves the norm bound.
[T]

The boundary census is therefore a count of resolved cross-cut
coupling strengths. The proof uses orthogonal idempotence. A generic
finite-temperature correlation matrix, or an oblique Riesz projector,
needs a modified statement.

Corollary 5.2 (filtered channel bound). Set \(F_\varepsilon=\mathbf1_{[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]}(C_A)\).
For any response dictionary \(\Phi\)
and any \(\tau>0\), \[
\boxed{\#\{\lambda_j(\Phi^\dagger F_\varepsilon\Phi)\ge\tau\}
\le\operatorname{rank}(F_\varepsilon\Phi)
\le N_\varepsilon(A)}.
\tag{5.4}
\] Proof. The Gram equals \((F_\varepsilon\Phi)^\dagger(F_\varepsilon\Phi)\)
and has the same rank. Its positive-threshold count cannot exceed that
rank. Its output range lies in \(\operatorname{Ran}F_\varepsilon\).

normalized afterward. The two thresholds have different purposes.

Overflow, a driven crossing, and the scalar rigidity law

The concentration is real within the finite model, but the light

A
conditional variational theorem, with the action stated

The scalar \(R_\eta\) from (2.3) is
a weak-transport diagnostic. To use it as a dynamical coefficient, an
effective action must be specified. Consider a locally planar interface
in a coordinate patch with a common Euclidean reference norm and the
model action \[
\mathcal S[q]=\int\left[\frac12R_\eta(q)|\dot q|^2-V(q)\right]d\tau.
\tag{5.5}
\] This reference norm specifies the scalar isotropic reduction
in that patch; it is not a derivation that the full intrinsic tensor is
Euclidean, nor an identification of \(q\) with spacetime. For anisotropic
intrinsic transport the full tensor action has to be retained. The
scalar action is a stated modelling assumption. [H]

Proposition 5.3 (interface matching for the scalar
action). Assume that the two regions have positive constant
coefficients \(R_1,R_2\), the interface
is stationary, the coefficients and potentials are translation-invariant
in its tangential direction, and the crossing path satisfies the
variational corner conditions. Let \(E>V_i\) be its common conserved energy,
and let \(\theta_i\) be its velocity
angle from the interface normal in the common reference norm. Then \[
\boxed{R_1(E-V_1)\sin^2\theta_1
=R_2(E-V_2)\sin^2\theta_2}.
\tag{5.6}
\] For equal potentials this reduces to \[
\boxed{R_1\sin^2\theta_1=R_2\sin^2\theta_2}.
\tag{5.7}
\] Proof. Translation invariance and
stationarity at the crossing give a common tangential canonical momentum
\(p_\parallel=R_i v_i\sin\theta_i\).
The autonomous action has \(E-V_i=R_iv_i^2/2\). Squaring the first
relation gives \(p_\parallel^2=2R_i(E-V_i)\sin^2\theta_i\)
on both sides. [T]

These angles are tangent-space velocity angles relative to an
interface. They are not defined merely by the principal angles of two
finite endpoint subspaces. An explicit path/coordinate map is required
to compare them with (3.9).



The scalar-action transmission branch for
\(R_2>R_1\). Angles are measured
from the interface normal. The coefficient is rigidity, not a count of
channels; the old labels equating greater rigidity with more channels
have been removed. Equation (5.7) requires the action and matching
assumptions above.

Substituting the authoritative definition, and writing \(s_i=\sigma_{\min}(A_{\mathrm{intr},i})\),
gives \[
\boxed{\sin^2\theta_2
=\frac{s_2^2+\eta}{s_1^2+\eta}\sin^2\theta_1}
\tag{5.8}
\] for equal potentials and a common regularizer. A real
transmission angle requires the right side to be at most one. When \(s_2\to0\) at fixed \(\eta\), the factor approaches \(\eta/(s_1^2+\eta)\), not zero. The collapse
toward a normal transmitted direction is a zero-regularizer limit with
\(s_1\) bounded away from zero. For
unequal potentials, the factor \((E-V_1)/(E-V_2)\) remains.

Optical
matching and the remaining constitutive test

At a stationary, unpatterned planar optical interface, tangential
phase matching in (4.11) gives \[
R_{\mathrm{opt},1}\sin^2\theta_1
=R_{\mathrm{opt},2}\sin^2\theta_2
\tag{5.9}
\] for propagating positive-index modes in the scalar/decoupled
TE setting. Here the coefficient has already been calculated from the
electronic response in Section 4.10. It is not defined using \(\sigma_{\min}(A_{\mathrm{intr}})\).

The two laws have the same form. Their identification requires the
two angle maps to agree and, over the intended class of media, \[
\frac{R_\eta(q_1)}{R_\eta(q_2)}
=\frac{R_{\mathrm{opt}}(q_1)}{R_{\mathrm{opt}}(q_2)}.
\tag{5.10}
\] Equivalently, \(R_\eta(q)=c_*R_{\mathrm{opt}}(q)\) with a
fixed calibration factor on that class. Agreement at one point does not
establish this relation. The input operator, its norms, physical probe,
and frequency dependence must be independently specified. This is the
remaining rigidity–constitutive identification, not a premise hidden
inside the theorem.

Proposition 5.1 (a specific calibration
obstruction). Consider the natural scalar field-Jacobian choice
\(\sigma_{\min}^2=g\ge0\), with fixed
\(\eta>0\) and optical law \(R_{\mathrm{opt}}=1+kg\), \(k>0\). Then \[
\frac{R_\eta}{R_{\mathrm{opt}}}
=\frac{1}{(g+\eta)(1+kg)}
\] is strictly decreasing in \(g\). In fact, for \(0\le g<h\) the denominator difference is
\[
(h-g)\bigl[1+k(h+g+\eta)\bigr]>0.
\] Consequently no fixed calibration can identify these two
coefficients at two distinct metric values. [T] This
excludes the specified field-Jacobian identification; it does not
exclude every transport operator or a separately specified device model.
Any proposed constitutive bridge must supply a different operator
dependence or additional physical structure and test it
independently.

The square-root
propagation threshold

For a lossless TE half-space, set \(D=R_{\mathrm{opt},2}-(k_\parallel/k_0)^2\).
Its outgoing normal wavevector is \[
k_{z,2}=k_0\sqrt D.
\tag{5.11}
\] The square root has the outgoing/decaying branch. With \(\zeta=k_0z\), the scalar equation \(u''+Du=0\) has a first-order
representation \[
\frac{d}{d\zeta}\begin{pmatrix}u\\u'\end{pmatrix}
=M(D)\begin{pmatrix}u\\u'\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M(D)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-D&0\end{pmatrix}.
\] Since \(M^2=-DI\), its
separated projectors are \[
P_\pm=\frac12\left(I\pm\frac{M}{\sqrt{-D}}\right),\qquad D\ne0.
\] At \(D=0\) the propagation
generator is defective; individual projectors become singular, while
\(P_++P_-=I\) remains regular.
[T] This is a spatial propagation generator, not an
electronic Hamiltonian and not the intrinsic metric. Evanescence for
\(D<0\) does not by itself mean
absorption.

For equal permeabilities and incident reference \(a=k_{z,1}/k_0>0\), the impedance ratio
is \(a/\sqrt D\). Its Smith coordinate
is \[
\Gamma(D)=\frac{a-\sqrt D}{a+\sqrt D}
=1-\frac2a\sqrt D+O(D),\qquad D\downarrow0.
\tag{5.12}
\] For \(D=-\kappa^2\), \(|\Gamma|=1\) and \(\arg\Gamma=-2\arctan(\kappa/a)\). The chart
stays finite but retains the square-root threshold. This derivation
holds \(a\) fixed; varying the
incidence angle also varies that reference.

5.3
Two boundary evolutions, not a universal classification of light

The source’s reflecting model is a finite periodic real skew matrix
\(H_{\mathrm{ref}}\). Consequently
\(e^{tH_{\mathrm{ref}}}\) is orthogonal
and preserves norm. Its directed comparison is instead \[
H_{\mathrm{one}}=-\lambda I+cN,
\] where \(N\) is a strictly
lower shift and is nilpotent. Then \[
e^{tH_{\mathrm{one}}}=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(ctN)^j}{j!}.
\tag{5.13}
\] All its eigenvalues are \(-\lambda\), and the polynomial transfer is
eventually dominated by the exponential decay. [T] The
source uses \(n=5,c=1,\lambda=1.2\) and
reports norm approximately \(2.6\times10^{-7}\) at time twenty.
[V]

The two models differ in their connectivity as well as damping.
Merely adding uniform damping to the periodic skew matrix would shift
imaginary eigenvalues into the left half-plane, not make them all real.
Neither diagram proves that every physical interface has only these two
outcomes. Photon condensation in a microcavity is a distinct
thermalizing physical realization [33], not a consequence of norm
preservation in the finite skew model.



Conceptual blocked and transmitted
continuations retained from the source illustration. They are hypotheses
about possible physical behavior, not an exhaustive theorem or a
numerical solution of the optical interface problem.



The actual algebraic comparison: a
periodic skew generator versus a directed nilpotent chain with uniform
decay. A bounded norm and an eventually decaying norm are proved for
these respective operators; the laboratory realization needs its own
model.

5.4 The
fixed-family ledger is even to every order

Let \(H(t)=H_0+tE\) with \(H_0^\top=H_0\) and \(E^\top=-E\). The matrix entries may be
complex for the transpose identity; the positive-shift formula below
assumes real matrices.

Theorem 5.4 (scalar parity). At every common
resolvent point, \[
\operatorname{Tr}(zI-H(-t))^{-1}
=\operatorname{Tr}(zI-H(t))^{-1}.
\tag{5.14}
\] Hence a contour ledger with the same admissible contour and
analytic function is even in \(t\).

Proof. \(H(-t)=H(t)^\top\), the inverse commutes
with transposition, and the trace of a transpose is unchanged. Integrate
the identical scalar integrands. Analyticity then implies that every odd
Taylor coefficient vanishes. [T]

For real \(H_0,E\), choose \(c\) with \(M=H_0+cI>0\), and set \(J=M^{-1/2}EM^{-1/2}\). The nonzero
eigenvalues of the real skew matrix \(J\) are pairs \(\pm i\omega_j\). Factoring \(M+tE=M^{1/2}(I+tJ)M^{1/2}\) gives \[
\boxed{Z(t)-Z(0)=\sum_j\log(1+t^2\omega_j^2)},
\qquad Z''(0)=\|J\|_F^2.
\tag{5.15}
\] The positive Hermitian part keeps the spectrum in the right
half-plane, so the principal trace-log is well defined and equals the
log determinant here. This proves the exact all-orders scalar response
for this fixed family, not merely the first-order cancellation. The
ledger is even, not constant.



The source tip–tail interpretation, with
its scope made explicit. Scalar parity is proved for fixed symmetric and
skew operators. Directed response can have odd terms, and
self-consistent intensity-dependent feedback is not solved by this
identity.

The physical tip–tail reading remains [H]. Allowing \(H_0\) or \(E\) themselves to depend on the driven
field requires a new symmetry check of the complete family. Replacing
transpose symmetry by Hermitian/skew-Hermitian adjoint symmetry
generally yields conjugation symmetry, not an even complex ledger; \(\log(1+it)\) is an elementary
counterexample.

5.5
What the local-geometric null gates do and do not exclude

The source fold and near-conical gates measure coverage by a fixed
angular fan in specified smooth local families. The reported
fold-to-control area ratios remain near one over the tested resolutions,
and the conical-family coverage remains near \(24.3\%\)–\(24.7\%\) as the tested domain shrinks.
These are numerical null results for those constructions.
[V]

They do not prove that no smooth family can ever produce another
scaling law. Higher-order degeneracy, nonuniform limits, global
arrangement, and different parameter-to-space maps are not exhausted by
two examples. The stronger universality assertion is removed from the
abstract and conclusion. A useful negative test identifies a mechanism
that failed under specified controls; it does not certify a theorem
about every mechanism not tested.



The original fold and conical coverage
plots. They are retained as finite-family null tests, not as a universal
no-compression theorem. The unchanged fold script was replayed
completely for this edition.

6. Does light exhibit the
Kakeya effect?

6.1 Three different questions

A geometric Kakeya set contains a unit segment in every direction. A
thickened tube problem studies the volume and overlap of neighborhoods
of such segments. A wave estimate controls a coherent superposition,
including its oscillation and interference. These questions are related,
but a theorem about tube unions is not itself a theorem about every
optical field.

Wang and Zahl establish full Hausdorff and Minkowski dimension for
Kakeya sets in three dimensions [24]. Guth, Wang, and Zhang prove the
sharp cone square-function estimate underlying the
two-spatial-dimensional local-smoothing result [25]. These are external
mathematical inputs, not consequences of the finite capacity gate.
[C] The particular critical three-spatial-dimensional
wave estimate below is still an open target in the primary-source
account [26]. A proof must supply a spacetime norm estimate, not just a
bound on a matrix rank.

6.2
A packet has a wavelength, a width, and a propagation length

Let a narrow-band paraxial packet have central wavenumber \(k_0\), transverse width \(w\), and angular spread \(\Delta\theta\). Fourier uncertainty gives
the scaling \[
\Delta\theta\gtrsim\frac{1}{k_0w}.
\] Keeping the angular spread within a comparable width over
propagation length \(L\) requires \(L\Delta\theta\lesssim w\), hence \[
\boxed{w^2\gtrsim L/k_0\sim\lambda L.}
\tag{6.1}
\] Constants depend on the definitions of width and bandwidth.
This is a paraxial scaling argument, not a universal compact-support
assertion. A plane wave has a direction but no finite transverse width.
A wavelength-scale focus is not automatically a long wavelength-width
tube. The usual wave-packet decomposition at frequency scale \(R\) uses angular scale \(R^{-1/2}\) and corresponding anisotropic
spatial scales [25].

The source illustration’s identity \(\delta=\lambda\) is therefore replaced by a
scale dictionary. In a tube estimate, \(\delta\) is the transverse tube width after
normalizing its length. In an optical realization, the bandwidth,
propagation length, aperture, polarization, and normalization must
determine that \(\delta\). None is
supplied by the name “needle.”



A direction family and a wave-packet
model, with width distinguished from wavelength. Geometric overlap and
coherent concentration are different observables. The scale relation
displayed is a paraxial estimate, not a claim that all optical packets
have the same shape.

6.3 The wave estimate
that would be needed

One critical Euclidean target in three spatial dimensions is \[
\left\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\right\|_{L^3(\mathbb R^3\times[0,1])}
\le C_\beta\|f\|_{W^{\beta,3}(\mathbb R^3)},\qquad \beta>0.
\tag{6.2}
\] The endpoint index and the positive derivative loss are part
of the statement [26]. The finite Gram, trace-log, or Schur identities
in this paper do not establish (6.2). In particular, scalar transpose
parity is not a bound on the absolute value of a spatially resolved
interference sum.

The original lattice dictionary uses normalized Gaussian directional
profiles with zero longitudinal carrier wavenumber in the executed gate.
It is an instrument for comparing projected shapes on a lattice, not an
already calibrated high-frequency Maxwell packet decomposition. Its
reproduction is evidence about that instrument. An optical comparison
must replace or derive the dictionary and keep the input-power and
output-noise normalization visible.

The constructive opportunity is specific: build a spatial response
operator, derive its singular-channel bounds at a controlled resolution,
and compare those bounds with a wave-packet norm estimate. The geometric
theorem remains an input. The census theorem says exactly which finite
channels it bounds; it does not license a claim that smooth geometry has
been excluded from every possible approach.

7. What approximation
and reduction retain

7.1
Normalization can change which information is stable

The clock-and-shift construction separates a branch-protected integer
from a normalized defect that can become small as the ambient dimension
grows. Results on almost commuting matrices in normalized
Hilbert–Schmidt distance [27,28] explain why a small dimension-averaged
error need not control an operator-norm obstruction.
[C] This is a statement about specified invariants and
approximation regimes, not a theorem that every integer is erased by
every normalized norm.

For permutation matrices \(U,V\) on
\(n\) points, direct counting gives
\[
\boxed{\|U-V\|_F^2=2n\,d_H(U,V).}
\tag{7.1}
\] Each differing column contributes two to the squared Frobenius
norm. The operator norm is not always two: relative transpositions
attain two, while a relative three-cycle has distance \(\sqrt3\) from the identity. One must
identify the actual relative permutation before asserting a norm
value.

Sofic and related approximation programmes concern coherent
approximate multiplication and separation requirements, not just one
norm identity. No conclusion about the existence of non-sofic groups is
used in this paper. The scalar regularized torsion and complete-complex
results from the companion permutation work are separate mathematical
problems. Their useful contribution here is the distinction between a
summed defect and the arrangement that produced it.

7.2
The displacement Gram and an exact projector realization

For a unitary \(U\), define \[
G_U=I-\frac{U+U^\dagger}{2}
=\frac12(U-I)^\dagger(U-I),\qquad K_U=G_U/2.
\tag{7.2}
\] Thus \(0\le K_U\le I\). For a
permutation, the trace of \(G_U\)
counts its moved points. On a cycle of length \(\ell\), \[
\lambda_j(G_U)=2\sin^2(\pi j/\ell),\qquad 0\le j<\ell.
\tag{7.3}
\] This follows by diagonalizing the cyclic shift in its discrete
Fourier basis. A two-cycle is represented by two opposite displacement
vectors: their Gram off-diagonal entry is \(-1\), not \(-1/2\). Formula (7.2) handles that case
without an ambiguous simple-cycle convention. [T]

Define orthonormal graph frames and their range projectors by \[
Q_U=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}I\\U\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q_I=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}I\\I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal P_U=Q_UQ_U^\dagger,\quad\mathcal P_I=Q_IQ_I^\dagger.
\] Since \(Q_I^\dagger
Q_U=(I+U)/2\), \[
\boxed{Q_U^\dagger(I-\mathcal P_I)Q_U=K_U.}
\tag{7.4}
\] The eigenvalues of \(K_U\)
are exactly the squared sines of the principal angles between these
graph subspaces. For two permutation matrices, \[
\boxed{d_H(U,V)=\frac1n\|\mathcal P_U-\mathcal P_V\|_F^2.}
\tag{7.5}
\] Indeed, the off-diagonal blocks of the projector difference
are \((U-V)/2\) and its adjoint. This
is an explicit map from a discrete permutation reading to
orthogonal-projector geometry. It is not a replacement for an oblique
Riesz projector.

The fixed normalization \(K_U=G_U/2\) is essential. For binary
entropy \(h(x)=-x\log
x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\) with \(h(0)=h(1)=0\), \[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}h(K_U)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U^2=I.}
\tag{7.6}
\] Proof. An eigenphase \(e^{i\phi}\) contributes \(\sin^2(\phi/2)\) to \(K_U\). Entropy vanishes exactly when each
such number is zero or one, equivalently every eigenphase is \(+1\) or \(-1\). Unitary diagonalizability then gives
\(U^2=I\). For a permutation on its
moved support, this is the transposition-only condition.
[T]

Normalizing each specimen by its own largest Gram eigenvalue does not
have this property: a three-cycle has Gram spectrum \(\{0,3/2,3/2\}\), which becomes \(\{0,1,1\}\). The repaired fixed
normalization instead gives \(\{0,3/4,3/4\}\) and positive entropy. This
correction is to the reading, not to the underlying displacement
construction.

7.3 The same
graph geometry has a Cayley coordinate

On \(\ker(I-U)^\perp\), where \(I-U\) is invertible, set \[
Z=(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}=iX,\qquad X=X^\dagger.
\] For an eigenphase \(e^{i\phi}\ne1\), \(X=\cot(\phi/2)\) in the same eigenbasis.
Hence \[
\boxed{K_U=(I+X^2)^{-1}},\qquad
U=(Z-I)(Z+I)^{-1}.
\tag{7.7}
\] The principal-angle threshold is therefore \[
\sin^2\theta\ge\varepsilon
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|x|\le\sqrt{\varepsilon^{-1}-1}
\tag{7.8}
\] mode by mode. The fixed subspace of \(U\) is a pole of this chart; it must be
retained separately rather than declared nonexistent. The displacement
Gram loses orientation: \(G_U=G_{U^\dagger}\). The full \(U\) or signed \(X\) retains information that the
squared-angle reading discards. This is a lossless algebraic Cayley
construction, not yet an identification of a permutation with an optical
scattering matrix.

7.4 Curvature before the
defect norm

Let \(U_{ij}\) transport the \(j\)-fiber to the \(i\)-fiber, with \(U_{ji}=U_{ij}^{-1}\). Define triangle
holonomy \(F_{ijk}=U_{ij}U_{jk}U_{ki}\)
and covariant differences \[
(d_U^0s)_{ij}=U_{ij}s_j-s_i,\qquad
(d_U^1t)_{ijk}=U_{ij}t_{jk}-t_{ik}+t_{ij}.
\] Expanding and canceling the terms at the first two vertices
proves \[
\boxed{(d_U^1d_U^0s)_{ijk}=(F_{ijk}-I)U_{ik}s_k.}
\tag{7.9}
\] Thus the local transports compose as a differential exactly on
flat triangles. For unitary transports, \[
\frac12\|U_{ij}U_{jk}-U_{ik}\|_F^2
=\operatorname{Tr}G_{F_{ijk}}.
\tag{7.10}
\] For permutation transports, summing the right side gives the
integer moved-point numerator in the companion complete-complex ratio.
Its vanishing has an exact compatibility meaning. It is not a
scattering-amplitude numerator, although both warn against studying only
a denominator.

The face holonomies satisfy an additional ordered identity: \[
\boxed{F_{012}F_{023}=U_{01}F_{123}U_{10}F_{013}.}
\tag{7.11}
\] Proof. Both sides reduce, by adjacent inverse
cancellation, to \(U_{01}U_{12}U_{23}U_{30}\). This holds in
any group. [T] The finite companion gate checks all
\(6^6=46{,}656\) assignments of \(\mathrm{Sym}(3)\) to the six edges. A
scalar norm of each face does not preserve the full ordered
identity.

7.5 Coherent composition
after a reduction

Let \(P\) now be a linear projection
on an associative algebra, not a band projector, and define \(x\star y=P(xy)\) on its image. Then \[
\boxed{(x\star y)\star z-x\star(y\star z)
=P\big[x(I-P)(yz)-(I-P)(xy)z\big].}
\tag{7.12}
\] Proof. Substitute \(P=I-(I-P)\) in the two inner
multiplications; associativity cancels \((xy)z-x(yz)\). Linearity gives the
displayed identity. [T] Information removed at an
intermediate product can contribute at a later product. For real
matrices with \(P(X)=(X+X^\top)/2\),
taking \(Z=\sigma_z\) and \(X=\sigma_x\) gives associator \(-Z\) for \((Z,X,X)\), an exact nonzero control.

Sullivan’s fluid-algebra discussion explicitly uses tree-indexed
homotopy corrections and connects their construction to Feynman trees
[30]. The homotopy-transfer treatment of tree scattering amplitudes
supplies a separate rigorous example [31]. Those published constructions
require a differential, compatible operations, and contraction data such
as \(pi=I\) and \(dh+hd=I-ip\). The nonflat operator in (7.9)
does not satisfy an ordinary \(d^2=0\)
premise. Its curvature cannot be discarded before applying such a
theorem. The associahedron organizes coherence; it does not turn an
arbitrary projected product into a coherent homotopy algebra by
itself.

Figueiredo and collaborators’ hidden-zero and numerator constructions
[29,35] motivate retaining cancellations and factorization data. Their
specific scattering theories are not identified with the finite
transport model here. Browder’s surgery perspective [34] likewise
motivates naming permissible modifications and their obstructions; a
finite gauge repair is not automatically a manifold-surgery
obstruction.

One additional square-root mechanism is worth separating from
refraction. Catalan composition satisfies \(C(t)=1+tC(t)^2\), so \(C(t)=(1-\sqrt{1-4t})/(2t)\). Polygon
triangulations and square-root field parametrizations meet through that
quadratic relation in [35]. A combinatorial branch and a propagation
threshold can share quadratic algebra without having the same physical
cause.

8. What
survives a wall, and what a boundary measures

8.1 Different
failures must have different names

The framework now distinguishes at least five objects: a pullback
metric losing rank; a smallest transport singular value tending to zero;
a spectral cluster meeting its contour; a numerator cancellation in a
specified response; and a loss of positivity in a separately defined
response or biorthogonal form. Their loci need not agree.

For the orthogonal-projector tensor of Section 3, positivity and the
metric–curvature inequality cannot fail while its hypotheses remain
true. A general real symmetric \(g\)
and real antisymmetric \(\Omega\) can
be combined into a Hermitian matrix \(Q=g-i\Omega/2\) that loses positivity even
when \(g\) remains positive. In two
parameters, that is tested by the diagonal conditions and \(\det g-\Omega_{12}^2/4\ge0\). But such an
independently assigned \(Q\) is not
automatically an orthogonal tangent Gram. A biorthogonal application
must define its form and verify this identification rather than infer it
from the same symbols.

The source’s assertion that a curve and a point cannot meet is also
too strong. Generic codimension statements require a specified family
and transversality hypotheses; intersections are possible. No
codimension slogan proves that a geometric wall and an exceptional point
are distinct in every model. The examples and their operators
decide.

8.2 Reciprocity: a
precise real-domain statement

Let \(B>0\) be real symmetric and
let \(V^\top B=BV\). Then \(BV\) is symmetric, and for any real vectors
\(x,y\), \[
x^\top BVy=y^\top BVx.
\tag{8.1}
\] For positive real \(N_i,N_j\)
and a nonzero real gap, the expression \[
\frac{(x_i^\top BVx_j)(x_j^\top BVx_i)}
{N_iN_j(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2}
\ge0
\tag{8.2}
\] is a real square divided by a positive number.
[T] This equality does not require the vectors to be
eigenvectors of a \(B\)-symmetric
generator.

It also does not identify (8.2) with every nonnormal
spectral-projector response. For a general nonnormal generator, left
eigenvectors need not be \(B\) times
its right eigenvectors. The actual residues and round-trip factors must
be calculated with the appropriate left/right pairing. A complex square
is not an order-positive real number merely because it is a square.



The reciprocity diagram, restricted to
the real symmetric-probe bilinear form that is actually proved.
Positivity of a true biorthogonal spectral response needs additional
hypotheses relating the left and right vectors.

8.3
Self-adjointness supplies a Gram, not every possible response

For an orthogonal projector tangent \(V\), write \(X=PV(I-P)\) and \(Y=(I-P)VP\). Self-adjointness gives \(Y=X^\dagger\). The metric is then a
positive Gram of cross-subspace blocks, as Section 3 proves. Breaking
this relation removes that proof of positivity; it does not compel a
negative eigenvalue in every non-Hermitian example.

Similarly, the physical marker Gram in Section 4 remains positive at
arbitrary mixed-state rank. A mixed marker can reduce duality saturation
without producing a negative Gram direction. “Loss of visibility,”
“degeneracy,” and “indefiniteness” are different events. The edition
retains the non-Hermitian programme while removing an unsupported
identification of these events.

8.4 The
contour condition is the continuation condition

Let \(M_\Gamma=\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\|(zI-H)^{-1}\|\)
and let \(\|E\|M_\Gamma<1\). The
Neumann expansion gives a resolvent on the same contour for \(H+E\) and \[
\sup_\Gamma\|(zI-H-E)^{-1}\|
\le\frac{M_\Gamma}{1-M_\Gamma\|E\|}.
\] The resolvent identity and the contour length bound give \[
\boxed{\|\Pi_\Gamma(H+E)-\Pi_\Gamma(H)\|
\le\frac{\operatorname{length}(\Gamma)}{2\pi}
\frac{M_\Gamma^2\|E\|}{1-M_\Gamma\|E\|}.}
\tag{8.3}
\] This is a local, contour-dependent stability theorem; it uses
a resolvent bound, not merely distance to eigenvalues of a nonnormal
matrix [2]. An analytic \(f\) has a
corresponding bound with \(\sup_\Gamma|f|\). [T]

A degeneracy of a parameter pullback metric need not affect any of
these hypotheses. Conversely, if the spectrum reaches the selected
contour, this representation ceases to be admissible. For a logarithmic
ledger, the analytic logarithm domain must also remain available. An
internal collision of eigenvalues within the enclosed cluster does not
necessarily destroy the cluster projector.

8.5 An
individual mode fails while the pair remains

For \[
H(\gamma)=\sigma_x+i\gamma\sigma_z,
\] the eigenvalues for \(0\le\gamma<1\) are \(\pm\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\).
Euclidean-normalized left and right eigenvectors of an individual mode
have overlap magnitude \(\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}\), and the corresponding
Petermann factor is \((1-\gamma^2)^{-1}\). The individual
spectral projectors are \[
P_\pm=\frac12\left(I\pm\frac{H}{\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}}\right).
\tag{8.4}
\] They become singular as \(\gamma\to1\), whereas \(P_++P_-=I\). A fixed contour enclosing the
pair and remaining off the spectrum retains rank two at the collision.
The entire finite matrix resolvent is bounded on a fixed compact such
contour near this finite collision; divergence of a shrinking or
single-mode contour is not a theorem that every admissible contour
diverges.

For a sufficiently large positive shift \(c\), the pair trace-log is \[
\operatorname{Tr}\log(cI+H)=\log(c^2-1+\gamma^2)
\tag{8.5}
\] on the common principal branch. It is smooth near \(\gamma=1\) but not constant. This separates
the invariant pair count from a varying scalar ledger and from singular
individual-mode amplitudes. [T]



The source individual-mode overlap and
pair-count example. The square-root collapse is an individual pairing;
the integer two is the projector rank of the whole isolated pair. The
trace-log is smooth, not generally constant.

The broader entropy and gravity comparisons in the earlier manuscript
are not used as proofs of this separation. An entanglement entropy, a
relative entropy, and a trace-log are distinct functionals. Connecting
any of them to a spacetime field equation requires its own state,
variation, normalization, and physical hypotheses. This paper confines
its entropy claims to the specified finite spectra and leaves broader
spacetime comparisons to separate work.

8.6 Schur
elimination and the exact boundary response

Let \(L\) be a finite complex
operator with \(\operatorname{Herm}L=(L+L^\dagger)/2\ge0\).
Partition interior and boundary indices, assume \(L_{ii}\) is invertible, and define \[
Z_b=L_{bb}-L_{bi}L_{ii}^{-1}L_{ib},\qquad
W=\begin{pmatrix}-L_{ii}^{-1}L_{ib}\\I\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8.6}
\] The lift \(Wb\) solves the
interior equation with boundary value \(b\). Since \(LW=(0,Z_b)^\top\), multiplication gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Herm}Z_b=W^\dagger\operatorname{Herm}L\,W\ge0.}
\tag{8.7}
\] No normality is needed. Positivity implies that \(Z_b+I\) is invertible: a vector with \(Z_bx=-x\) would have negative real
quadratic form unless \(x=0\).

Use a real positive reference normalization and set \(V_b=(Z_b+I)^{-1}\) and \(S_b=(Z_b-I)V_b\). Expanding the difference
\((Z_b+I)^\dagger(Z_b+I)-(Z_b-I)^\dagger(Z_b-I)\)
proves \[
\boxed{I-S_b^\dagger S_b
=4V_b^\dagger\operatorname{Herm}Z_bV_b
=4(WV_b)^\dagger\operatorname{Herm}L\,(WV_b).}
\tag{8.8}
\] [T] For an input \(a\), this is the exact quadratic-form
identity \[
\|a\|^2-\|S_ba\|^2
=4(WV_ba)^\dagger\operatorname{Herm}L\,(WV_ba).
\tag{8.9}
\] It retains how the interior modes meet a specified drive. It
is a pullback of a response form, not a proof that this form equals the
intrinsic tangent metric. The response is contractive, but power not
returned through the selected ports can include transmission to other
ports rather than irreversible absorption.

Positive eigenvalues alone do not suffice. For \(Z_b=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&4\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
both eigenvalues are one, yet \(S_b=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
has largest singular value two. The Hermitian part is indefinite,
exactly the missing hypothesis.

For real \(M>0\), real skew \(E\), and \(L_t(\omega)=M+tE-i\omega I\), the Hermitian
part stays \(M>0\). For the same
partition, transposition also gives \[
Z_b(-t)=Z_b(t)^\top,\qquad S_b(-t)=S_b(t)^\top.
\tag{8.10}
\] Consequently diagonal responses are even, but \(S_{ij}-S_{ji}\) is odd and can have a
first-order term. Scalar ledger parity does not mean all observable
directional response has no first-order change.

Operator-graph language gives another exact reading. With \(\mathcal W=(I,S_b)^\top\) and \(\mathcal J=\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)\),
\[
\mathcal W^\dagger\mathcal J\mathcal W=I-S_b^\dagger S_b.
\tag{8.11}
\] Contractivity is positivity of the ambient indefinite form on
the response graph. This is a useful point of contact with Nag–Sullivan
operator-graph geometry [32], but a period-map interpretation requires
additional symplectic and analytic structure. A common graph coordinate
does not identify its various possible metrics.

8.7
Surface coupling, absorption, and the Smith coordinate

To make one physical boundary realization explicit, assume a local,
infinitely thin electric sheet of independent dipoles at normal
incidence, with identical lossless media on each side. Let its
conductivity be \(\sigma_s=-i\omega
N_s\alpha(\omega)\) for time dependence \(e^{-i\omega t}\), and write \(\zeta=Z_0\sigma_s\). Tangential electric
continuity and the magnetic jump give \[
t=1+r,\qquad -2r=\zeta t.
\] Thus \[
r=-\frac{\zeta}{2+\zeta},\qquad t=\frac2{2+\zeta}.
\tag{8.12}
\] Looking into the outgoing medium plus the sheet gives
normalized impedance \(z_b=1/(1+\zeta)\), whose Smith transform is
exactly \(r=(z_b-1)/(z_b+1)\). The
power fractions satisfy \[
\mathcal R=\frac{|\zeta|^2}{|2+\zeta|^2},\quad
\mathcal T=\frac4{|2+\zeta|^2},\quad
\mathcal A=\frac{4\operatorname{Re}\zeta}{|2+\zeta|^2},\quad
\mathcal R+\mathcal T+\mathcal A=1.
\tag{8.13}
\] For a passive sheet \(\operatorname{Re}\zeta\ge0\). In this
particular symmetric one-sided electric-sheet model, \[
\frac12-\mathcal A
=\frac{(\operatorname{Re}\zeta-2)^2+(\operatorname{Im}\zeta)^2}
{2|2+\zeta|^2}\ge0.
\tag{8.14}
\] The one-half bound is a conditional matching result. A backing
mirror, two coherent inputs, magnetic response, or unequal media changes
its hypotheses. The model shows explicitly why no reflection does not
imply complete absorption: \(\zeta=0\)
has \(r=0\), \(t=1\), and \(\mathcal A=0\).

Microscopic surface electronic response can also require information
beyond a bulk permittivity. Feibelman response functions describe such
boundary corrections; broadband measurements at a gold–air interface
provide a recent example [36]. [C] These functions
describe induced charge/current response, not the position of a single
surface electron. The sheet and bulk models in this paper do not imply
that electrons exist only at a surface or that all refraction consists
of real photon absorption and delayed re-emission.

8.8 A numerator retains
the probe relation

For fixed input \(b\), output \(a\), and \(z\) off the spectrum, \[
T_{ab}(z)=a^\dagger(zI-H)^{-1}b
=\frac{a^\dagger\operatorname{adj}(zI-H)b}{\det(zI-H)}.
\tag{8.15}
\] The denominator records the possible spectral poles. The
numerator also records the probe relation. At a simple eigenvalue \(E_j\), comparison of residues gives \[
N_{ab}(E_j)=D'(E_j)\,a^\dagger P_jb.
\tag{8.16}
\] An uncoupled spectral mode can have its possible pole canceled
in that response. For Hermitian \(H\)
and \(a=b\), the residue is \(\|P_jb\|^2\).

Take \(H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}c&s\\s&-c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
with \(c^2+s^2=1\) and \(b=e_1\). Then \(D(z)=z^2-1\) while \(T_{bb}(z)=(z+c)/(z^2-1)\). A numerator zero
moves at fixed eigenvalues. This is an exact finite response example,
not a claim that its numerator is the canonical form of a particular
amplitude geometry.

The scalar ledger can regain probe sensitivity through an explicit
insertion. For \(M>0\), the
determinant lemma gives \[
\frac{\det(M+tbb^\dagger)}{\det M}=1+t\,b^\dagger M^{-1}b,
\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\log\det(M+tbb^\dagger)\right|_{t=0}
=b^\dagger M^{-1}b.
\tag{8.17}
\] A scalar without a probe discards orientation. Its response to
a specified probe need not. This identity connects the determinant,
resolvent, and boundary-response instruments without conflating them.
[T]

9. Discriminating
measurements and model tests

A physical test must measure the operator or response used in the
theorem, with its units and thresholds fixed in advance. The following
programme keeps the original experimental direction but separates the
distinct tests.

Marker response. Vary the trap state, recoil, and
mixing while estimating visibility and operational distinguishability.
The mixed-state marker Gram remains positive by Section 4, so a negative
direction would concern a different constructed form or a violated
modeling assumption. A shortfall in duality saturation is not by itself
an independent intrinsic-licence boundary. The thermal-marker
calculation remains a model prediction, not an additional laboratory
measurement.

Boundary-filtered channels. Measure or reconstruct
the region projector, its cross-cut coupling, and the specified input
dictionary. Determine the transition-window filter independently of the
measured channel count. Report the two thresholds \(\varepsilon,\tau\), raw output strengths,
noise floor, and power calibration. Renormalizing every filtered output
would erase the suppression the census theorem is supposed to measure. A
structured-light aperture experiment needs a physical implementation or
derivation of this filter; a finite matrix rank bound does not supply
that implementation.

Electronic geometry and optical response. Hold
transition energies approximately fixed while varying the electronic
eigenstates relative to a fixed dipole/polarization probe. Measure
response strengths, not only peak positions. Below the gap, test the
positive-weight nullspace prediction; between resonances, distinguish a
tune-out zero from a spectral gap closure. In a many-body or dissipative
material, use the appropriate response theory rather than importing the
independent finite-dipole assumptions unchanged.

Rigidity and refraction. Reconstruct \(A_{\mathrm{intr}}\) in fixed, declared
domain and range inner products. Choose one physical normalization and
one regulator procedure, then predict rigidity ratios without fitting
them to measured angles. Independently obtain \(R_{\mathrm{opt}}\) from the optical
response. The hypothesis that the scalar transport law describes the
optical interface requires compatible coordinates and an independently
justified proportionality of the coefficients across the compared
states. A parameter-space principal angle is not automatically a
physical incidence angle. The cap \(R_\eta\le1/\eta\) and the order of
regularizer/threshold limits must be reported.

Recycling and feedback. Compare a plain emitter, a
conventional recycling cavity, and the proposed structured/non-Hermitian
architecture at equal input energy and thermal constraints. The figure
of merit is useful output per input energy, including losses and
external pumping. A passive return loop cannot supply net energy from
the difference between two mathematical readings. Any improvement must
come from redistribution, storage, extraction, or reduced dissipation
within an explicit energy balance. The intensity-generated wall and the
fixed-family parity theorem do not yet solve the nonlinear feedback
dynamics. [H]

These tests can challenge the proposed physical identifications
without invalidating identities that remain true for their stated
mathematical objects. Conversely, a passing numerical gate does not
establish a physical identification it never tested.

10. Future work

The finite results proved here define the scope of this paper. Three
extensions motivate further research; none is a premise of the
projector, census, boundary-response, or finite constitutive
results.

10.1
Physical identification of transport rigidity and optical response

Retain \(R_\eta=[\sigma_{\min}(A_{\mathrm{intr}})^2+\eta]^{-1}\)
with its specified inner products and regularizer. A physical
identification with \(R_{\mathrm{opt}}\) requires an
independently derived transport operator, probe coupling, units, and
parameter-to-space map, followed by a test of proportionality over the
intended class of states. Agreement of two squared-Snell equations alone
does not supply this map. Proposition 5.1 excludes the particular scalar
choice \(\sigma_{\min}^2=g\) with fixed
positive \(\eta\) and \(R_{\mathrm{opt}}=1+kg\), \(k>0\); it leaves other independently
specified constructions open.

Related questions concern the measurement of a biorthogonal response
form and conditions under which actual nonnormal spectral pairings
inherit probe reciprocity. These require their own response maps; the
physical mixed-marker Gram remains positive.

10.2 Continuum optical
directional capacity

The finite direction geometry and filtered census bound are
established. Extending them to a universal optical aperture bound
requires a physical response dictionary, a realizable boundary filter,
fixed normalization and thresholds, and a controlled continuum limit. A
spatial wave estimate must relate those data to wavelength, packet
width, and propagation distance. Any proposed local-smoothing
consequence must address the norm estimate in (6.2) directly.

The fold and conical gates are finite-family null tests. A general
geometric exclusion would need hypotheses covering additional
degeneracies, global arrangements, and nonuniform limits. The continuum
scaling and its prefactor remain future targets, while the exact finite
bound retains its present scope.

10.3 Long-lived light-source
dynamics

The architecture calls for a closed emitter–field–medium model with
stated input power, useful optical output, internal losses, and thermal
response. Its feedback equations must be solved for existence and
stability rather than inferred from fixed-family ledger parity. A
meaningful test compares performance against a conventional cavity under
matched input, coupling, and loss conditions. Distributed conservative
emitter operation and thermal management can be incorporated as
engineering choices in that comparison.

The programme includes testing whether nonnormal response improves
useful extraction or redistributes stress, and separating those effects
from transient amplification or instability. Predicting perceptual color
additionally requires the outgoing spectrum and an observer model.
Neither the finite identities nor the calibration obstruction decide the
architecture’s engineering outcome.

Further formalization of spectral differentiation, analytic contours,
and the connecting dynamical arguments can accompany these extensions.
The current declaration-level proof scope is recorded in Appendix B.

11. Conclusion
The finite response results now give a direct way to ask whether silence is coupling lost or contributions cancelled. Two distinct squared photon energies in one pole-free interval recover the metric kernel through a positive response difference. A single tune-out can hide active transitions; two exact scalar zeros in that interval cannot, under the stated lossless assumptions. Probe-span certificates separately identify which tensor readings are determined and which perturbations remain invisible. These additions strengthen the finite measurement programme without supplying the three physical extensions reserved above. [T]

The central distinction survives the stricter formulation: local
geometry, spectral continuation, and measured response are different
readings of related operator data. A pullback metric can lose rank
without a spectral contour becoming inadmissible. An individual mode can
become singular while the projector of its entire isolated cluster stays
regular. A scalar ledger can be even or isospectrally constant while a
directed, probe-dependent response changes.

The paper establishes five concrete components: orthogonal-projector
geometry; the exact cross-boundary census and its filtered-channel
bound; the Schur–Cayley interior-to-boundary response identity; the
transition-resolved connection between electric-field geometry and
optical polarizability; and exact tune-out and nullspace results. The
response weights preserve the metric’s real kernel below the gap, while
positive-residue transitions admit a unique lossless two-pole tune-out
between resonances. Equal total metrics can nevertheless have unequal
static response. Rigidity has a controlled regularizer, resolvent
representation, finite stability bound, and conditional scalar-action
refraction law.

The relational information cannot all be compressed to eigenvalues, a
determinant, a defect norm, or a list of directions. Probe couplings,
holonomy order, boundary maps, and coherent composition retain different
parts of it. The permutation and homotopy-algebra connections are useful
precisely when their maps and hypotheses are stated, and cease to be
useful when shared notation is mistaken for a proof of physical
equivalence.

Light and matter provide the organizing physical interpretation.
[H] The mathematical contribution is the finite
operator framework and its explicit response relations, supported by
reproducible model calculations and 91 verified light-specific Lean
declarations. Physical rigidity–optical identification, continuum
directional capacity, and the long-lived source architecture are the
three future directions in Section 10. The present results stand under
their displayed hypotheses independently of how those extensions
develop.
