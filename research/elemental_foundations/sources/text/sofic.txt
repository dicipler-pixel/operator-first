                                        The Soficity Trace
                 What was tried, what failed, what survived, and what it cost

                                             Jeromie Beasley

                                             23 August 2026


                                                  Abstract
          A complete working record of one attempt to bring the operator-first winding currency to
      bear on the existence of non-sofic groups. It contains the failed gate at full size, the theorem
      hidden inside the failure, the repaired gate and its eight control blocks, the exact conversions
      between the four metrics that the question lives on, the three results that went into the projector
      geometry paper — integrality and invariance
                                           √         of the winding, the admissible domain δ + g 2 ≤ 4,
      and the amplitude floor |w| ≤ (d/4) δ — the standing verdict that the route does not reach
      soficity and exactly why, and the routes killed and left open. Every claim carries a grade: [T]
      derived, [V] gated numerically, [C] cited, [H] interpretation. Nothing that was killed has been
      deleted.


1    The situation
On 1 August 2026 OpenAI announced ten machine-verified results from an unreleased model, Astra.
The third is the construction of a non-sofic group, closing a question posed by Gromov in 1999 and
named by Weiss in 2000 [C]. A group is sofic if it can be approximated by finite permutations: for any
finite set of elements and any ε, there is a map into a symmetric group that is almost-multiplicative
(composition agrees on at least 1 − ε of the points) and almost-free (any non-identity element moves
at least 1 − ε of them). The metric is normalised Hamming — the fraction of points on which two
permutations differ.
     Audit of the released repository, run 20 August: all ten formalisation files carry zero sorry,
zero axiom declarations, and zero native decide — the last matters, because it is the standard
way a zero-sorry proof still escapes the kernel. The forty-two sorries in the repository are confined to
a separate challenge-stub directory and are not part of the proofs. This is a claim about a repository
at a date, not a mathematical theorem, and it is recorded as one [V for the mechanical audit]. What
a certificate cannot settle is whether the formalised statement is the intended one; that surface is
unaudited [H].
     Follow-up, checked directly:

    • Fournier-Facio, arXiv:2608.02025 (3 August). Takes the key technical criterion and produces
      a finitely presented torsion-free non-sofic group, avoiding Leavitt algebras and Thompson’s V
      entirely, by small cancellation over a relatively hyperbolic pair. The stated point of the note
      is that the criterion, not the construction, is the novelty [C].

    • Alekseev–Thom, arXiv:2608.05362. The centraliser of a sofic embedding of a Kazhdan group
      is itself a metric ultraproduct of permutation groups; an ergodic centraliser forces LEF, and
      finite presentation then forces residual finiteness [C].

                                                      1
    • Kun–Thom, arXiv:2608.06222. Generalises the mechanism: for Γ < G with the commensurator
                                                                                     L
      of Γ generating G, both Kazhdan, Γ not normal, the generalised wreath product ( G/Γ Z/2)⋊G
      is non-sofic — on explicit pairs of residually finite Kazhdan elementary matrix groups over
      polynomial rings. Also separates weakly sofic from sofic [C].

   As of the date of this trace nothing further has appeared, and the two siblings, MF and
hyperlinear, remain open [C]. Both halves of that sentence are dated on purpose.


2     The map onto the framework, before any verdict
Stated first because the comparison is worth making in earnest, and because several of the corre-
spondences are exact rather than suggestive. Where a correspondence is exact it is derived; where
it is a translation into this framework’s vocabulary it is labelled as one.

2.1   The two clauses
The two sofic clauses are the framework’s two. Almost-multiplicative is cocycle composition. For
almost-free the sofic condition is a Hamming condition: a non-identity element’s approximating
permutation must move almost every point. On the framework side the analogue is a uniform
principal-angle separation, sin2 θ ≥ ε — distinct directions stay resolvable [H]. Compose correctly,
and do not lose anybody. The translation is the right one, and it is a translation, not a definition of
soficity.

2.3    The DGLT door, and which rung it is on
De Chiffre–Glebsky–Lubotzky–Thom (2017; published 2020) built the first non-approximable groups
from central extensions 1 → Z → G   e → G → 1 with the obstruction a class in H 2 (G; Z). Their norm
                                                                                                   √
is the Frobenius norm, ∥T ∥F = ( ij |Tij |2 )1/2 — the unnormalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm, d
                                     P
times the normalised one [C]. That is not this framework’s metric natively. Ambient size does not
drop out of it, which is exactly the opposite of the zero-and-span normalisation, and it is the reason
a counterexample exists on that rung while the hyperlinear question — the same norm, normalised
— has been open since Connes.

                                                           3
    The structural rhyme survives the correction, and is worth keeping for what it is. A central
extension is a phase; the obstruction class is a winding no approximation can unwind [H]. What the
correction removes is the claim that the door was already open onto this framework’s own floor. It
was one rung up.

2.4    Property (T) is a spectral gap
The trivial representation is isolated; there are no almost-invariant vectors. Its role in killing soficity
is a rigidity argument: approximation is forced near something exact which cannot be there. That
is a wall in the framework’s sense [H].

2.5    The honest blocker, stated after the map
The framework’s instruments live in finite dimensions with isolated spectrum: Gram determinants,
                                  √
principal angles, contours at η ≳ ε. Sofic approximations live in an ultraproduct of symmetric
groups where the spectrum is a measure and there is nowhere to put a contour. Second, and
structurally worse: the diagnostic is always given an operator family and asks whether it tracks.
Soficity asks whether any approximating family exists. That is a quantifier flip, and persistence of a
transport one has says nothing about non-existence of transports one does not.


3     WIND-GATE-01 — built, run, failed
3.1    The design
One closure defect, two readings. Amplitude δ = ∥R−I∥2F /d. Phase w = (1/2π) times the unwrapped
total change in (1/d) arg det Rm over m = 0, . . . , d. Testbed: clock and shift in dimension d, relator
R = ABA−1 B −1 = ω −1 I, a genuine 2-cocycle, with δ = 4 sin2 (π/d) → 0.

3.2    What passed, and it is the half worth keeping
P1, exact control: PASS. δ = 1.9 × 10−32 , w = 0.
    P2, noise control: PASS. δ from 2.4 × 10−1 down to 2.3 × 10−7 across η = 10−1 . . . 10−4 , with
w = 0.000000 at every level. The null side is clean: the gate does not manufacture obstructions out
of small amplitude defects.

3.3    What failed
P3: FAIL. δ reproduced exactly — 4 sin2 (π/d) to every digit, a 715× drop from d = 3 to d = 97 —
but w = 0 at every d.
    P4: FAIL. w(cR) ̸= w(R). Since det(cRm ) = cm det(Rm ), the phase shift is m-dependent and
does not cancel. Fails zero-and-span outright.
    P5: FAIL. A matched-δ pair returned w = 0.103322 — not an integer. A winding needs a
closed path; Rm for m = 0, . . . , d closes only when Rd = I. The unperturbed clock–shift closes, the
perturbed one does not, so arg det simply accumulates as mθ. An integer was asserted and closure
was never gated.




                                                    4
3.4   The special-case manifestation
det(ζId ) = ζ d , and by Schur an irreducible projective representation whose commutator has order p
has dimension exactly p, so ζ d = 1 always. In the dimension where the projective representation
lives, the determinant line is structurally blind to a scalar 2-cocycle [T]. No finite-dimensional
projective representation has a cocycle that det can see. This is exactly why DGLT needed an
approximation norm and a cohomological argument rather than a determinant: everyone who tries
the obvious phase invariant hits this wall. The risk was flagged during design and then talked past;
it should have been gate one, not gate three.

3.5   The general diagnosis
The blindness above is true and it is not the root cause. The root cause is the path. The determinant
of a commutator is identically 1, so the endpoint of any commutator carries exactly zero information,
and no adjustment of dimension repairs that. The correct loop is Kazhdan’s, rediscovered by Exel
and Loring in 1991: the chord t 7→ det((1 − t)I + tR), t ∈ [0, 1], which closes precisely because
det = 1 at both ends. Its winding number equals the summed principal arguments of the eigenvalues
of R — the quantity physicists now call the Bott index [C].
    The relation between the two readings is one line: det = exp Tr log, so the determinant collapses
the summed principal logarithms modulo 2πi. It discards exactly one wrap, and the wrap is the
entire invariant.


4     WIND-GATE-02 — repaired, run, passed
4.1   Why the check proves what it claims, stated before the result
For clock and shift, R = ωI with ω = e2πi/d . Summing the principal logarithms of the d eigenvalues
gives d · (2πi/d) = 2πi, so w = 1. Taking the determinant instead exponentiates that sum and
collapses it to ω d = 1. The two differ by one wrap. The check therefore has a known exact answer at
every d, which makes the run a calibration against a fixed point rather than a discovery: anything
other than 1 on the first block means the instrument is broken, not the mathematics. What makes
it worth running at all is that δ = 4 sin2 (π/d) → 0 while w stays pinned at 1 — an amplitude dying
while an integer persists, with an answer known in advance to check against.

4.2   The results
Theorem 1 (Integrality and invariance; T+V).PLet g = minj |λj + 1| > 0, where λj are the
eigenvalues of R = U V U † V † , and let w = (1/2π) j arg λj on the principal branch. Then w ∈ Z
with |w| ≤ d/2; w is unchanged by simultaneous unitary conjugation; w is unchanged by U 7→ eiθ U
and V 7→ eiφ V ; and w equals the winding number of t 7→ det((1 − t)I + tR).
                                                              Q              P
Proof. R is a commutator of unitaries, so det R = 1, hence j λj = 1 and j arg λj ∈ 2πZ. The
bound follows from | arg λj | < π. Conjugation preserves the spectrum. The scalars cancel identically
inside the commutator. The chord closes because det R = 1 at t = 1 and det I = 1 at t = 0; the
identification of its winding with the summed principal arguments is Exel–Loring [C].

   The determinant of the relator is identically one. That triviality is not an obstacle to reading
the record; it is the reason the record is an integer.

5     The standing verdict
This route does not establish non-soficity, because the invariant it carries is stable in a stronger
metric than the one soficity tests.
    The Bott–Exel–Loring winding obstructs approximation in the operator norm. The testbed
is the historical witness for exactly that: Voiculescu’s 1983 counterexample to the operator-norm
question for almost-commuting unitaries is clock and shift [C]. Descend one rung and the obstruction
is not merely weaker, it is provably absent: with respect to the normalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm,
almost-commuting unitaries are near commuting ones, dimension-independently. Glebsky (2010)
proves it for self-adjoint, unitary and normal matrices, and for several almost-commuting matrices
at once; Filonov–Kachkovskiy (2010) give the self-adjoint case independently, Hadwin–Shulman
(2018) place it inside the general tracial-stability framework, and Ioana (2024) supplies the negative
counterpart for general matrices [C]. In the currency of this trace: on the normalised rung, Z2 is
stable, so there is nothing there for a winding to obstruct.
    That is the correction to the earlier phrasing that the reason was simply “the metric.” The
reason is sharper. The winding currency is native to operator-norm and K-theoretic stability, where
quantitative index separation is established; the transfer to normalised Hamming has no stability or
separation theorem behind it, and one cannot be manufactured from the conversion dH = 12 ∥ · ∥2HS,n
alone. Arzhantseva and Păunescu make the same point from the other side: although the Hamming
distance is expressible through the Hilbert–Schmidt distance, the Hilbert–Schmidt techniques for

                                                   10
unitaries do not deliver commutator stability for permutations, which is why that result had to be
proved separately [C]. An exact conversion between two metrics does not carry theorems across
them.
   Rigidity on the wrong axis is blindness, not an asset [T].
What does the work over there, and it is not in this corpus. Kun’s theorem shows that the
sofic approximation graphs of a group with property (T) are essentially vertex-disjoint unions of
expanders. An expander has no sparse cut. Therefore the defect cannot be pushed into a thin seam
and made to vanish by fraction. That anti-localisation step is the load-bearing piece of the whole
construction [C]. Any programme aimed at “defect confinement” is re-deriving Kun 2016.


6     Two outside readings, assessed
Reading one restated the winding position correctly and raised two cautions, both of which were
accepted. First, “same currency in two realisations” is the right conceptual compression, but the
explicit normalisation identifying the smooth loop integral with w on a common family is not
established, and was overstated; it is carried at [H] in the paper. Second, calling the boundary a
rigidity wall is interpretation, not a consequence of the identity alone. Where the reading stopped
short: it treated δ + g 2 = 4 as a fact about clock and shift. It is the equality case of a general bound,
and the general bound is the theorem — which is what converts the wall from interpretation into a
proved domain statement.
    Reading two proposed that the Hamming metric lets a topological defect escape to a vanishing
boundary — a seam of size L in an area L2 — and proposed an experiment to watch it happen.
The instinct is right and the vintage is wrong. Its literature framing describes the state before 1
August. More to the point, the anti-seam mechanism it says is missing was proved in 2016: property
(T) is exactly the condition preventing defect localisation, by Kun’s expander theorem. Of its three
proposed outcomes, the third is the true one and it is already a theorem. The proposed experiment
also hides its whole difficulty in one step — “translate the defect into a permutation action” —
when the shift is a permutation matrix and the clock is not, and the impossibility of carrying that
phase by permutations is the entire problem. The cited research note, read directly, is more careful
than the use made of it: it states outright that no non-sofic group has been constructed there and
isolates the remaining global statement as an open conjecture.


7     The chiral routes
7.1    The lead
If the medium tracing the currents is itself chiral — a Dirac operator, a chiral nematic, a non-Abelian
vortex condensate — then the invariant it carries is not norm-valued and may survive the local
surgery that Hamming forgives. The literature supports the class. Annala, Zamora-Zamora and
Möttönen construct knots and links of non-Abelian vortices topologically protected against local
reconnections and strand crossings, with the protection proved by an invariant of links coloured
by the quaternion group Q8 , realisable in dilute Bose–Einstein condensates [C]. The December
2025 chiral-nematic experiment tracks a conserved Hopf index through knot fusion and fission [C].
Helicity, being abelian, leaks through reconnections and is the wrong member of the family.
    This is the correct class of invariant for a metric that forgives local cut-and-paste, and it is
genuinely different from anything the gate measures. The two branches of the lead fail differently,
and only one of them fails.


                                                   11
7.2   The Q8 colouring branch: closed
A Q8 colouring is a homomorphism from the complement’s fundamental group into Q8 up to
conjugacy. Any invariant built by valuing or counting homomorphisms into a fixed finite group
factors through the profinite completion, since a map to a finite group extends uniquely to it. That
is why it survives surgery: it reads only the finite quotients, and local cut-and-paste does not change
which finite quotients exist.
    Now consider where soficity is in doubt. Residually finite implies sofic. LEF implies sofic. The
load-bearing piece of the construction is a finitely presented infinite simple group — trivial profinite
completion, no finite quotients at all — and the argument’s punchline is that if the container were
sofic then that piece would be LEF, which is impossible because finitely presented LEF groups are
residually finite.
    So on the only groups in play, every finite-group colouring invariant is identically trivial.
Robustness, profiniteness and blindness are one property under three names [T]. The colouring
survives every local move for the same reason it cannot see anything.
    Confirmation from the other side: the invariant that does work in the approximation story is
second bounded cohomology, and it is provably not a profinite invariant — there exist pairs of
residually finite groups with isomorphic profinite completions, one with vanishing and one with
non-vanishing real second bounded cohomology [C]. Non-profiniteness is the price of separating
power.

7.3   The Hopf branch: open
The Hopf index is not a finite-group colouring. It is integer-valued, defined from a map to a target
whose homotopy carries Z, and it does not automatically factor through any profinite completion.
The kill of the previous subsection has no purchase on it, and applying it there would be a category
error.
    What blocks this branch is different and weaker: no realisation of the index on the finite
approximants has been constructed. Robustness under local surgery is free, but separation is not —
the invariant must be computable on the approximating permutation systems and take a value the
group cannot match, and nobody has built that map. The obstruction is

                      no finite-approximant realisation has been constructed,

not
                                         profinite blindness.
The first is an absence of construction and can be repaired by construction. The second is a theorem
and cannot [H for the assessment, C for the ingredients].

7.4   Why the two failures are different, and why it matters
Distinguishing them is not bookkeeping. A programme that treats both branches as killed by
profiniteness will stop looking, and the thing it stops looking for — an integer-valued, surgery-stable
invariant with a finite-approximant realisation — is precisely the shape of what the sofic metric
would need. The Q8 branch is closed by a theorem about what finite-group invariants can see. The
Hopf branch is open and unattempted.

8     The two borrowed methods, taken seriously
8.1   Piccirillo, and the figure-eight slice
The trace move is not “swap in an equivalent object”. It is: determine what the invariant actually
depends on, discover that the dependence is coarser than the object, then move to any representative
where it is computable. Sliceness depends only on the 0-surgery trace, not the knot; find another
knot with the same trace where the s-invariant is computable. All the leverage is in the coarseness
of the dependence.
    The figure-eight work in this corpus is the same move in a different category, and it is worth
stating in those terms. The mixed Hessian at a singular point of the A-polynomial curve does not
depend on the whole curve. Palindromicity in the longitude, L2 A(1/L, M ) = A(L, M ), means it
depends only on behaviour along the fixed locus of the involution L 7→ 1/L. On that locus,
                                    ALM (±1, M ) = ±AM (±1, M )
exactly, so the mixed partial vanishes at every singular point lying there — all four nodes at once
rather than the two the original argument reached [T].
    What the slice revealed was two things. Negative: the apparent second-order degeneracy is not
second order at all, because “M is a repeated root of A(1, M )” and “AM = 0” are the same equation,
so the phenomenon is an artifact of the amphichiral symmetry. Positive: the involution is the
amphichirality; the tangent cones are axis-aligned in the canonical meridian–longitude coordinates
for that reason; and at (−1, ±1) the cones are definite, making those points acnodes.
    Applied to the present problem, the same dependence analysis asks what soficity depends on.
Not the presentation: soficity is closed in the space of marked groups, so non-soficity is open there
(Ceccherini-Silberstein–Coornaert, Prop. 7.5.10) [C] — and openness is the trace-analogue, and it
was cashed immediately — finite presentation came for free, while torsion-freeness required separate
work precisely because torsion-freeness is not open. Any candidate invariant must be a function of
the marked-group germ. That rules out a great deal before construction begins.

8.2   Viazovska, and where the certificate already is
The method has three parts, not one. Recognise the bound as linear programming over a positivity
cone. Know the dual certificate’s exact sign conditions — the function and its Fourier transform each
with prescribed signs beyond a radius. Construct it from a structure carrying the right symmetry,
which is where modular forms enter in dimension eight.
    The defect-floor statement one would want — nontrivial class implies defect fraction at least
c > 0 — is a linear statement about the empirical distribution of disagreement, so a dual certificate
exists in principle. But the sharper observation is that on one side the certificate already exists
and it is the Kazhdan constant. For a group with property (T), every unitary representation
without invariant vectors has some generator moving every unit vector by at least κ. That constant
plays a role structurally analogous to the dual certificate’s: it forces a floor without inspecting
any particular approximation, and it is why the expander theorem exists at all. Symmetry gives
an explicit constant which forces the bound — the same architecture in both places [H for the
identification, C for the ingredients]. It is an analogy between two mechanisms, not a claim that
one is an instance of the other.
    The frontier is therefore not “find a certificate”. It is: find one for groups without (T), where no
symmetry hands you κ. That is well posed, and it is the one place the corpus has a native variable,
since the sin2 θ ≥ ε threshold is a Kazhdan-constant-shaped object — a uniform angular separation
floor.

import numpy as np

TAU = 1e-3
rng = np.random.default_rng(20260820)

def clock_shift(d):
    w = np.exp(2j*np.pi/d)
    U = np.diag([w**k for k in range(d)])
    V = np.roll(np.eye(d, dtype=complex), 1, axis=0)
    return U, V

def commuting_pair(d):
    U = np.diag(np.exp(1j*rng.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, d)))
    V = np.diag(np.exp(1j*rng.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, d)))
    return U, V

def relator(U, V):
    return U @ V @ U.conj().T @ V.conj().T

def unitary_kick(U, eta):
    d = U.shape[0]
    H = rng.normal(size=(d, d)) + 1j*rng.normal(size=(d, d))
    H = (H + H.conj().T)/2
    H /= np.linalg.norm(H, 2)
    ev, W = np.linalg.eigh(H)
    return U @ (W @ np.diag(np.exp(1j*eta*ev)) @ W.conj().T)

def readout(R):
    d = R.shape[0]
    lam = np.linalg.eigvals(R)
    g = np.min(np.abs(lam + 1.0))
    delta = np.linalg.norm(R - np.eye(d), 'fro')**2 / d
    w = np.sum(np.angle(lam)) / (2*np.pi)
    return lam, g, delta, w

def winding_chord(R, nt=20001):
    lam = np.linalg.eigvals(R)
    t = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, nt)[:, None]
    ang = np.unwrap(np.angle((1.0 - t) + t*lam[None, :]), axis=0)
    return np.sum(ang[-1] - ang[0]) / (2*np.pi)
