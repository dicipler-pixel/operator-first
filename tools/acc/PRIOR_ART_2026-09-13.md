## 1. Exact certificates, bottleneck (minimax) distance, length caps — CLOSEST PRIOR ART

- Josep Carreras, *Machine-checkable equivalence certificates at the length-14 Andrews–Curtis
  frontier*, arXiv 2607.23611 (26 Jul 2026); code at github.com/joe-carr-data/ac-certificates (MIT,
  read). Four explicit AC-equivalences among Shehper et al.'s six hard length-14 presentations as
  elementary-move certificates replayed by a dependency-free stdlib verifier; exhaustive minimax
  analysis of the substitution-move graph: bottleneck distance MS(3, y x² y) → AK(3) is exactly 19,
  and any path from an MS(2) representative to AK(3) must reach total length ≥ 27.
- Overlap with this lane: our length-well / capped-closure results (ac-00015 first descent needs
  ceiling 27, second well closed through cap 30 with 9,096,912 states; ac-00002 cap-33/34 wells) are
  the same kind of statement (exact minimax over a length-capped move graph) on different
  instances. His "≥ 27" is for MS(2) → AK(3) under substitution moves; ours is for ac-00015 under
  the 14 official elementary moves. Not the same theorem, same discipline. Cite him when we publish
  any bottleneck claim; his verifier and ours (official SAIR verifier) are independent.
- Status of our work relative to his: our AK(3)-disguise identifications (ac-00399, ac-06279,
  ac-06945) and well closures are new instances, not a new method.

## 2. Search heuristics, greedy vs RL, growth — PRIOR ART EXISTS

- Shehper et al., *What makes math problems hard for reinforcement learning: a case study*,
  arXiv 2408.15332 (NeurIPS 2025): greedy search beats BFS and PPO; length-reducibility of all but
  two AK presentations; Miller–Schupp subfamilies resolved; topological data analysis of the
  landscape. Our beam negatives (length-first greedy failing on long k=1 records) are consistent with
  their finding, not a contradiction of it.
- Math-AI Caltech, *The Two-Hump Problem*, arXiv 2606.21611 (ICML 2026), code Math-AI-Caltech/ACSolverX:
  substitution supermoves (splice cyclic relators so ≥ 1 letter cancels), Dual-Ring Transformer with
  cyclic positional encodings, PPO solving 153 more presentations; "two-hump" hardness distribution
  (trivial vs impossible, few hard-but-solvable). Our war-room finding "training solutions are
  near-monotone descents; the scored pool needs growth the training set does not show" is the same
  gap seen from the data side. Their cyclic-relator insight is prior art for any "relators are
  rings" claim we make (our conj_sig / core vocabulary is a rotation-invariant signature — same idea).
- Miasnikov & Myasnikov, genetic algorithms (math/0304306); Havas–Ramsay BFS with secondary
  storage; Lisitsa automated reasoning (AITP 2019); Panteleev–Ushakov conjugacy-search framing
  (1609.00325): all prior search-heuristic work on AC.

## 3. Penultimate corridor (one relator a single letter) — ELEMENTARY, KNOWN; OUR MEASUREMENT IS NEW DATA

- Mathematics: if r0 = x^{±1}, every x-letter of r1 can be removed by AC moves (rotate the letter to
  the end by single-letter conjugations, cancel it by multiplying with r0^{∓1}); exponent sum of y in
  r1 is preserved, so r1 → y^{k} with k = exp_y(r1), and the abelian invariant forces k = ±1. The
  finish from the corridor is therefore always bounded by O(|r1|) moves. This is standard folklore
  (background: Myasnikov–Myasnikov–Shpilrain math/0304305, Myasnikov & Myasnikov "On the
  Andrews–Curtis equivalence"). Not ours.
- New data (this branch, `corridor_finish_v1.py`, exact BFS capped at L_entry + 2, every spliced
  path re-verified by the official verifier): all 424 training runs enter the corridor; the official
  tail after entry is longer than the exact shortest capped finish in 359/424 runs (65 equal, 0
  longer), 950 moves saved in total, largest single saving 8 (ms-train-0333: tail 17 → 9). So the
  official training solutions are not shortest paths from the corridor. Relevant to the efficiency
  leaderboard Tao's post describes (see §6). No prior publication of this statistic found.
- The "ProofAtlas penultimate-relator obstruction" pointer from the war room: the phrase occurs
  nowhere in this repository or its atlas. Unconfirmed; do not cite.

## 4. Smith chart / Cayley transform as a display of search dynamics — NO PRIOR ART FOUND

- The map Γ = (Z−1)/(Z+1) is P. Smith's 1939 chart (Möbius transform of the right half-plane onto

  discrete move graph, stacked by run fraction, appears in none of the searches (queries on Smith
  chart / Cayley transform + search dynamics / optimization trajectory returned only RF material).
- Claim we may make: "no prior use found in these searches", not "novel". The idea is the author's;

## 5. Snell's law in path search — PRIOR ART EXISTS (different setting)

- Mitchell & Papadimitriou, *The weighted region problem*, J. ACM 38(1) 1991: shortest paths through
  weighted planar regions obey Snell's law at region boundaries ("continuous Dijkstra"). Mata &
  Mitchell path-net approximation; Lara "Snell's law and light traveling along the shortest path".
- Our test (total length as refractive index, arg Γ as the angle) is a different, discrete setting
  and FAILED (5.4% of steps within 0.5). Recorded as a refuted prediction; the channel stays logged
  per the never-remove-eyes rule. Nothing to claim here beyond the negative result.
