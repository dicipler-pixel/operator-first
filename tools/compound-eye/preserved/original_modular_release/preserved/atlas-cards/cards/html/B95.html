B95L4 → L5 theorem

The spectral threshold profile reads the cycle type.

Cspec(ε) = ∑j #{k : sin²(πk/Lj) ≥ ε}
0 < ε ≤ 1; count eigenvalues of G/2, where G is the unit-displacement Gram matrix

A permutation defect moves points, and each moved point supplies a unit displacement (eπ(i) − ei)/√2. Their Gram matrix G splits over the nontrivial cycles. A cycle of length L contributes eigenvalues 2 sin²(πk/L), so G/2 gives the threshold profile written above. At a fixed number of moved points, transpositions contribute one unit eigenvalue per pair: their profile is flat over the whole interval (0, 1]. Every longer cycle contributes an eigenvalue strictly between zero and one, and therefore an interior step. A limited threshold window can miss that step. This is an exact spectral count, not a count of pairwise separated rays. A three-cycle makes the distinction visible: its three rays have pairwise squared projective separation 3/4, but G/2 has only two positive eigenvalues, both 3/4. At ε = 0.7, all three rays are separated while the spectral count is two. The profile depends on cycle type and ignores relabeling. Placement demands are another readout; the two together have not been proved to classify every arrangement.
