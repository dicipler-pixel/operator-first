B79L5 → L1 theorem

When the vector is unavailable, take the phase.

no eigenvector at the collision  ·  1⁄2π Δ arg d(λ)  still an integer
the discriminant is a scalar built from the operator alone; its argument winds whether or not any eigenvector exists

At a collision there is no eigenvector to carry, and normalising one is the step that fails: the pairing closes, the condition number diverges, and a gauge choice becomes arbitrary. The move is to stop asking for the vector. Build a scalar out of the operator that is blind to everything the vector is not — a discriminant, a determinant, the trace of a resolvent — and take the winding of its argument around the parameter loop. It is an integer, it needs no basis, and it is defined precisely where the eigenvector is not. Every integer record on these sheets that survives a wall was obtained this way, and the reading is the same each time: what cannot be carried as a direction can still be carried as a phase.
